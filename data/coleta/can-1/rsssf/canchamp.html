Canada - List of Champions

Amateur Era

While "football matches" were played before at various locations (Toronto 1859, New Westminster 1862 and Victoria 1865), the first game played under "London Association Rules" (referring to the FA established in London in 1863) took place in 1876, when teams from the Toronto Lacrosse Club and the Carlton Cricket Club met on Parliament Street in Toronto. See also "Ontario: The Early Years" at the Canadian Soccer History site for further information.
A first association, the Dominion Football Association, was formed in Toronto in 1877 and may be considered the first national football association outside of the British Isles, as its name indicated its intention to govern the game in the entire Dominion of Canada, but its membership appears to have been restricted to the Toronto area and it disappeared after some three years. Instead, in 1880 the Western Football Association (WFA) was formed in Berlin (now called Kitchener) in Ontario. Its activity concentrated on western and southern Ontario but it played an important role in the development of the game, not in the least by its regular interaction with the American Football Association, based in Newark, New Jersey, and a tour to Britain in 1888. One of its member clubs, Galt FC, represented Canada at the (unofficial) football tournament at the 1904 Olympics in Saint Louis, easily trouncing both US American opponents.
Meanwhile, other state football federations had been formed (e.g. in Manitoba in 1896, a second association in Ontario (the Ontario FA, currently the Ontario SA, in 1901), and in Alberta and Saskatchewan in 1906) and a first, unofficial, national competition, that for the People's Shield, was inaugurated in 1906.
Following the formation of further state associations in British Columbia (1910) and Quebec (1911), the Dominion of Canada Football Association (DCFA) was founded in 1912 and affiliated to FIFA in 1913; in the same year, the competition for the Connaught Cup, the first truly national football competition, was held in Fort William (now part of Thunder Bay). See Dave Litterer's file on the Canadian National Challenge Cup for an overview of finals for the Connaught Cup, Challenge Trophy and Carling Cup since 1913.

Professional Era - Voyageurs Cup

Between 2002 and 2007 this cup was awarded by the Voyageurs Group (a fan association) to the Canadian USL-1 (formerly A-League) team with the best record in the intra-Canadian matches.
It was handed over to the CSA to be awarded to the winners of the qualification tournament for the CONCACAF Champions League, dubbed Canadian Championship (bar sponsor name). Therefore, since 2008 the winners are considered the official Canadian champions.
