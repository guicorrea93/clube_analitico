The '''Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1''', formerly known as the '''Tunisian National Championship''' (1955–1994), is the highest-level [[association football|football]] competition in [[Tunisia]] and is organised by the [[Tunisian Football Federation]] (FTF) and the [[National Professional Football League (Tunisia)|National Professional Football League]] (LNFP). The history of the competition is somewhat complex, with the first edition being held in 1907 during the [[French protectorate of Tunisia|French protectorate in Tunisia]], organised by the [[Union des Sociétés Françaises de Sports Athlétiques|Union of French Athletic Sports Societies]] (USFSA), it was played in a knockout format and the first official match taking place on 9 June 1907. In 1921, the [[Tunisian Football Association League]] (LTFA) was founded, it is the Tunisian branch of the [[French Football Federation]], which remained in operation until independence in 1956.

On 29 March 1957, the Tunisian Football Federation was founded, the governing body for [[football in Tunisia]]. The Tunisian National Championship became professional in the [[1994–95 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|1994–95 season]] following the establishment of the National Professional Football League, and the competition took its current name, making it the first professional league in Africa and the Arab world. The top two teams in the final standings qualify for the [[CAF Champions League]], and the third-place team qualifies for the [[CAF Confederation Cup]]. In 2019, the Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1 ranked first in the Arab world and Africa, and fifteenth globally, according to the [[IFFHS]] rankings.

[[Espérance Sportive de Tunis]] has won  34 titles. Coach [[Faouzi Benzarti]] has won the championship on eleven occasions with Espérance de Tunis (5), [[Étoile Sportive du Sahel|Étoile du Sahel]] (4) and [[Club Africain]] (2), while former player [[Khalil Chemmam]] has won the championship on twelve occasions all with Espérance de Tunis.

==History==
=== Pre-independence (1904–1956) ===
Football was born in [[Tunisia]] in 1904 with the unofficial creation of the Racing Club de Tunis, formalized in 1905 but which had to wait for the creation of other clubs in [[Tunis]] and [[Bizerte]] to participate in an official competition. Then the government is obliged to take an active interest in it. It distributed a few grants, though very small. A committee of the [[Union des Sociétés Françaises de Sports Athlétiques]], responsible for the management of all sports in Tunisia, was then created. In 1910, a first series championship was organized with the participation of the Racing Club, Sporting Club de Tunis, Lycée Carnot de Tunis, the Colonial School of Agriculture, the Football Club of Tunis and the Red Star Club de l'Ariana, and a second series made up of the second teams of Racing Club de Tunis, Sporting and Gallia Club. The national title is awarded after a final between Racing and Stade maritime de Bizerte (champion of the Mediterranean squadrons) with a score of 2–0.

There are then very few Tunisian player. But, surprisingly, ''[[La Dépêche tunisienne]]'' published on 12 June 1910 the list of players of the Khereddine Club team, called to play against the Ariana Club and who are all Tunisians. It is the first fully Tunisian team but which we have not heard from later and which precedes the Comète Club team, created in 1914 but which did not survive the First World War. The Racing Club won the championship in 1909–10, 1910–11 and 1913–14,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Tunisia 1910/11 |url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablest/tun1911.html |access-date=2025-05-18 |website=www.rsssf.org}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Tunisia 1913/14 |url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablest/tun1914.html |access-date=2025-05-18 |website=www.rsssf.org}}</ref> then in 1919–20 and 1920–21, while Sporting de Ferryville was crowned in 1911–12 and 1912–13. The championship became official with the creation of the [[Tunisian Football Association League]] in 1921 was established, which is the Tunisian branch of the [[French Football Federation]]. Until 1939, the title of champion was awarded following play-offs between regional champions. From 1946 to 1947, a championship of "excellence" (national division) is created and is contested at the national level. In 1944–45, 1945–46 and 1952–53, the championship was not contested and replaced by a criterium (a sort of group tournament where participation was not compulsory). These years saw the creation of the [[Stade Tunisien]] in 1948.

=== Post-independence (1956–present) ===
Following Tunisia's independence on 20 March 1956, a profound reorganization of national football took place. The main clubs associated with French colonialism, such as Racing Club de Tunis, Sporting Club de Tunis, Stade gaulois, and Avant-garde, were dissolved. On 29 March 1957, the [[Tunisian Football Federation]] was founded, which has administered Tunisian football ever since, and the championship began to be played under its auspices. The [[1955–56 Tunisian National Championship|first season]] after independence saw [[CS Hammam-Lif]] crowned champion,<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1955/56 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun56.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> followed by [[Étoile Sportive du Sahel]], [[Club Africain]], and [[Espérance Sportive de Tunis]]. In the [[1956–57 Tunisian National Championship|1956–57 season]], [[Stade Tunisien]] won its first title,<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1956/57 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun57.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> while in 1957–58, Étoile du Sahel secured its second championship.<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#58 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> Espérance won its first post-independence titles in the 1958–59 and 1959–60 seasons,<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#59 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#60 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> quickly establishing itself as one of the dominant teams. In 1960, the [[Tunisian Super Cup]] was created. Stade Tunisien continued its winning streak, becoming champions in 1960–61 and 1961–62,<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1960/61 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun61.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1961/62 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun62.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> and the first team to achieve the double (league and [[Tunisian Cup]]) after independence. However, a dramatic event marked this period: on 20 March 1961, President [[Habib Bourguiba]] ordered the dissolution of Étoile Sportive du Sahel due to riots instigated by its fans. After a year's absence, the club returned triumphantly in the 1962–63 season, winning both the league and the cup, thus achieving its second double of the independent era.

During the 1960s, Club Africain won its first post-independence titles in 1963–64 and 1966–67.<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1963/64 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun64.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#67 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> Stade Tunisien added another championship in 1964–65,<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1964/65 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun65.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> consolidating its dominance in this decade with four titles. Étoile du Sahel was crowned champion again in 1965–66,<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#66 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> while [[Sfax RS|Sfax Railway Sport]] achieved its first post-independence title in 1967–68,<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#68 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> following its victories in 1933–34 and 1952–53.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Tunisia 1952/53 |url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablest/tun53.html |access-date=2025-05-18 |website=www.rsssf.org}}</ref> The 1968–69 season marked the first championship for [[CS Sfaxien]], securing the second consecutive title for the city of [[Sfax]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1968/69 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun69.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> The 1970s began with Espérance's fourth title in 1969–70.<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#70 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> CS Sfaxien won its second post-independence championship in 1970–71,<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1970/71 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun71.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> while Étoile du Sahel won in 1971–72.<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1971/72 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun72.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> Club Africain secured two consecutive titles in 1972–73 and 1973–74,<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#73 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1973/74 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun74.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> and Espérance added three more championships in 1974–75, 1975–76, and 1981–82.<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1974/75 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun75.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#76 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#82 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> A notable event was the 1976–77 coronation of [[JS Kairouan]], breaking the dominance of the larger clubs.<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1976/77 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun77.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> CS Sfaxien won in 1977–78, a season in which the Tunisian Cup could not be completed due to the [[Tunisia national football team|national team]]'s participation in the [[1978 FIFA World Cup]]. Club Africain closed out the decade with two consecutive titles in 1978–79 and 1979–80 seasons.

The 1980s were characterized by the alternating title wins of the four major Tunisian football clubs. CS Sfaxien won his fourth and fifth title in 1980–81 and [[1982–83 Tunisian National Championship|1982–83]],<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1980/81 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun81.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1982/83 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun83.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> while Espérance de Tunis dominated with four titles in 1981–82, 1984–85, 1987–88, and 1988–89, achieving their tenth championship. Étoile Sportive du Sahel secured three consecutive or nearly consecutive titles in 1985–86 and 1986–87,<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#86 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1986/87 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun87.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> reaching their seventh championship. CA Bizertin won their fourth title in 1983–84, demonstrating that other teams could also compete for the title.<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia - List of Final Tables |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tunhist.html#84 |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref> Club Africain closed out the decade with their ninth championship in 1989–90.<ref>{{cite web |title=Tunisia 1989/90 |url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablest/tun90.html |access-date=9 July 2021 |website=rsssf.com |language=en}}</ref>

== Media coverage ==
[[File:Est_cshl.JPG|thumb|253x253px|Match between [[Espérance Sportive de Tunis|Espérance de Tunis]] and [[CS Hammam-Lif]] during the [[2008–09 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|2008–09 season]].]]

The broadcasting rights for the Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1 are held by several television and digital media operators. In Tunisia, the public broadcaster [[Tunisian Television]] provides traditional television coverage of the championship through its channels [[El Watania 1]] and [[El Watania 2]]. Digital coverage is also provided by Diwan Sport, which obtained the digital broadcasting rights to the league for three seasons, including the rights to stream championship matches online.<ref>{{cite web |title=Diwan Sport obtient les droits digitaux de la Ligue 1 pour trois saisons |trans-title=Diwan Sport obtains the digital rights to Ligue 1 for three seasons |language=fr |website=SportBusiness.tn |date=5 September 2025 |url=https://www.sportbusiness.tn/diwan-sport-droits-ligue-1-tunisie/}}</ref> Internationally, the Qatari broadcaster [[Al Kass Sports Channels]], through its digital platform Shoof, holds broadcasting rights for a selection of approximately 30 matches per season, including coverage for audiences in the Gulf region.<ref>{{cite web |title=FTF : Qui a le droit de diffuser les matchs ? |trans-title=FTF: Who has the right to broadcast the matches? |language=fr |website=La Presse de Tunisie |date=17 September 2025 |url=https://www.lapresse.tn/2025/09/17/ftf-qui-a-le-droit-de-diffuser-les-matchs/}}</ref> The combined value of the main broadcasting rights is estimated at approximately 8.83 million Tunisian dinars per season.<ref>{{cite web |title=Football tunisien : le vrai prix des droits TV |trans-title=Tunisian football: the real value of television rights |language=fr |website=SportBusiness.tn |date=5 October 2026 |url=https://www.sportbusiness.tn/football-tunisien-prix-droits-tv-ligue-1/}}</ref>

== Trophy ==
[[File:6 egle 2020.jpg|thumb|300x300px|Current Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1 trophy since the [[2019–20 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|2019–20 season]].]]
According to the competition regulations, the [[Tunisian Football Federation]] adopts a new championship trophy every time a club wins the title for three consecutive seasons. Five cups have been adopted in the history of the competition since independence. The first trophy, a lion stretching out on a tree trunk, was adopted in the 1957-58 season, the first season organized by the Tunisian Football Federation. The first team to hoist it was [[Étoile Sportive du Sahel]], and many other teams have won it, including [[Espérance Sportive de Tunis]], [[Stade Tunisien]], Étoile Sportive du Sahel, [[Club Africain]], [[CS Sfaxien]], [[Sfax RS]], and [[JS Kairouan]]. 

It remained in use until the [[1999–2000 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|1999–2000 season]], after Espérance Sportive de Tunis won the championship for three consecutive seasons. The second trophy is a cup in the shape of an open-winged eagle standing on a football, mounted on a green marble base, and imported from Italy. Remarkably, Espérance Sportive de Tunis is the only team to have hoisted this cup and retained it so early, having won the championship for three consecutive seasons. The third trophy, also in the form of a closed-winged eagle centered on a wooden base, was imported from Italy and has been used since the [[2003–04 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|2003–04 season]]. It was first worn by Espérance Sportive de Tunis and won by other teams, including Étoile Sportive du Sahel, CS Sfaxien, and Club Africain. Espérance Sportive de Tunis retained it after winning the championship for three consecutive seasons.

The fourth trophy, an iron pole with a bronze football and two iron handles on top, was imported from France and adopted since the [[2011–12 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|2011–12 season]]. It was first worn by Espérance Sportive de Tunis and won by other teams, including Étoile Sportive du Sahel, CS Sfaxien, and Club Africain. Espérance Sportive de Tunis retained it after winning the championship for three consecutive seasons. The fifth emblem, adopted since the [[2019–20 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|2019–20 season]], is an eagle with outstretched wings. This emblem is distinguished by its Tunisian design and manufacture. It weighs 17 kg, is 14 cm thick, is 50 cm long, and is 88 cm wide. The trophy is made of bronze and coated in 18-karat gold.

== Format ==
As the [[2026–27 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|2026–27 season]], the Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1 consists of sixteen clubs, each playing each other home and away. The champion is the team with the most points after the 30 matchdays. At the end of the season, the champion and runner-up qualify for the [[CAF Champions League]], the third-placed team and the winner of the [[Tunisian Cup]] qualify for the [[CAF Confederation Cup]], and the bottom three teams are relegated to [[Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 2]] the following year. The standings are calculated using the following points system: a win is worth three points, a draw one point, and a loss earns no points.

In the event of a tie, the following tiebreakers are used:

# Higher head-to-head record;
# Higher goal difference in head-to-head record;
# Higher goal difference overall;
# Higher number of goals scored.
# Fair Play Ranking;
# Play-off match between the two teams.

==Records and statistics==
===Titled Managers===
{{main|List of Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1 winning managers}}

Over the course of 71 seasons, 40 coaches have managed to win the title. [[Faouzi Benzarti]] has won the tournament on a record eleven occasions with [[Espérance Sportive de Tunis|Espérance de Tunis]] (5), [[Étoile Sportive du Sahel|Étoile du Sahel]] (4) and [[Club Africain]] (2), while [[Youssef Zouaoui]] have won the title on five occasions with [[Espérance Sportive de Tunis|Espérance de Tunis]] (4) and CA Bizertin (1).

==Top scorers==
{{Main|List of Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1 top scorers}}

[[File:AMa10.png|thumb|218x218px|[[Ali Maâloul]] of [[CS Sfaxien]] is the [[2015–16 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|2015–16 season]] top scorer with 16 goals.]]
[[File:Haythem Jouini.jpg|thumb|270x270px|[[Haythem Jouini]] of [[Espérance Sportive de Tunis|Espérance de Tunis]] is the [[2012–13 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|2012–13 season]] top scorer with 8 goals.]]
The day after Tunisia's independence in 1956, the newspaper ''Le Petit Matin'' took charge of establishing the classification of the top scorer in the Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1. The newspaper Al Amal took over in 1961 then it was ''[[L'Action Tunisienne]]'' which formalized the classification and endowed it with a price from 1967, in parallel with the weekly Le Sport. Then, with the development of the media and the coverage of Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1 matches, this ranking becomes more known.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Tunisia – List of Topscorers|url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablest/tuntops.html|access-date=15 June 2020|website=[[RSSSF]]}}</ref>

==All-time table==
{{Main|All-time Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1 table}}

The classification of the Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1 table all seasons combined is a classification which aims to determine which team in the history of the Tunisian football championship has had the most success, not by the number of titles but by the number of points. This ranking combines all the points and goals of each team that has played in the Tunisian championship since independence in 1956 until the end of the [[2025–26 Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1|2025–26 season]]:{{#lsth:All-time Tunisian Ligue Professionnelle 1 table|General classification}}

== African and international competitions ==
=== Results in African and intercontinental competitions (CAF and FIFA) ===

[[File:Espérance Sportive de Tunis wins CAF Champions League.jpg|thumb|Espérance Sportive de Tunis, winner of the [[2018 CAF Champions League]].]]

Tunisian teams are among the best African teams with a total of 24 continental titles.<ref name="+2">{{cite web|language=en|title=African Cups - All-Time Tables|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afrcl-alltime.html|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref> Twelve Tunisian teams in total have played in African competitions, with [[Étoile Sportive du Sahel|Étoile du Sahel]] being the one that has won the most African competitions with nine titles, followed by [[Espérance Sportive de Tunis|Espérance de Tunis]] with eight titles.<ref name="+2"/> In the former [[FIFA Club World Cup]] format, now reclassified as the [[FIFA Intercontinental Cup]], Tunisian clubs have participated four times: three for Espérance de Tunis in [[2011 FIFA Club World Cup|2011]], [[2018 FIFA Club World Cup|2018]] and [[2019 FIFA Club World Cup|2019]] (thanks to their [[CAF Champions League]] titles in [[2011 CAF Champions League|2011]], [[2018 CAF Champions League|2018]] and [[2018–19 CAF Champions League|2019]])<ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=FIFA Club World Cup 2011|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesf/fifa-wcc2011.html|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref><ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=FIFA Club World Cup 2018|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesf/fifa-wcc2018.html|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref><ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=FIFA Club World Cup 2019|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesf/fifa-wcc2019.html|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref> and one for Étoile du Sahel, which finished fourth in [[2007 FIFA Club World Cup|2007]],<ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=FIFA Club World Cup 2007|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesf/fifa-wcc07.html|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref> the best result for a Tunisian team in this competition. In the new [[FIFA Club World Cup]], Espérance de Tunis participated in the [[2025 FIFA Club World Cup|2025]] edition, where they were eliminated in the group stage.

In the [[CAF Champions League]], Tunisian clubs occupy a prominent place in the competition. Espérance de Tunis has the most participations in the competition (25). They reached the final eight times, with four victories in [[1994 African Cup of Champions Clubs|1994]], [[2011 CAF Champions League|2011]], [[2018 CAF Champions League|2018]] and [[2018–19 CAF Champions League|2019]].<ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 1994|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup94.html#cc|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref><ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 2011|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup2011.html#cc|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref><ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 2018|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup2018.html#cc|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref><ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 2018/19|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup2019.html#cc|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref>

As for Étoile du Sahel, they participated thirteen times and reached the final three times (one victory in [[2007 CAF Champions League|2007]]);<ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 2007|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup07.html#cc|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref> [[Club Africain]] participated ten times with a victory in [[1991 African Cup of Champions Clubs|1991]],<ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 1991|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup91.html#cc|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref> while [[Club Sportif Sfaxien|CS Sfaxien]], with four participations, had to settle for second place in [[2006 CAF Champions League|2006]]. In the [[CAF Confederation Cup]], Tunisian clubs are the most successful in the history of this competition with five titles. CS Sfaxien is the most successful team with three titles in [[2007 CAF Confederation Cup|2007]], [[2008 CAF Confederation Cup|2008]] and [[2013 CAF Confederation Cup|2013]],<ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 2007|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup07.html#confed|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref><ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 2008|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup08.html#confed|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref><ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 2013|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup2013.html#confed|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref> followed by Étoile du Sahel with two titles in [[2006 CAF Confederation Cup|2006]] and [[2015 CAF Confederation Cup|2015]].<ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 2006|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup06.html#confed|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref><ref>{{cite web|language=en|title=African Club Competitions 2015|url=http://www.rsssf.com/tablesa/afcup2015.html#confed|website=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=6 July 2021}}.</ref> As for the [[CAF Super Cup]], thirteen participations yielded only three victories: two for Étoile du Sahel in [[1998 CAF Super Cup|1998]] and [[2008 CAF Super Cup|2008]] (runners-up in [[2004 CAF Super Cup|2004]], [[2007 CAF Super Cup|2007]] and [[2016 CAF Super Cup|2016]]) and one for Espérance de Tunis in [[1995 CAF Super Cup|1995]] (runners-up in [[1999 CAF Super Cup|1999]], [[2012 CAF Super Cup|2012]], [[2019 CAF Super Cup|2019]] and [[2020 CAF Super Cup|2020]]); CS Sfaxien participated three times in [[2008 CAF Super Cup|2008]], [[2009 CAF Super Cup|2009]] and [[2014 CAF Super Cup|2014]], including once against Étoile du Sahel in 2008.

===Best finish in Arab competitions by club===
Tunisian clubs are the second most titled Arab clubs with 11 championships (after Saudi clubs who won 12 championships). [[Espérance Sportive de Tunis|Espérance de Tunis]] is the most titled Tunisian club in the Arab championships with 4 championships, followed by [[Club Sportif Sfaxien|Club Sfaxien]] and [[Stade Tunisien]] with two championships each.

Tunisian teams won the most prestigious Arab championships, [[Arab Club Champions Cup]] 7 times, 3 of which were for the Tunisian [[Espérance Sportive de Tunis|Espérance de Tunis]], which has the record for the number of trophies in [[1993 Arab Club Champions Cup|1993]], [[2008–09 Arab Champions League|2008–09]] and [[2017 Arab Club Championship|2017]], two championships for the [[Club Sportif Sfaxien|Club Sfaxien]] in the [[2000 Arab Club Champions Cup|2000]] and [[2003–04 Arab Champions League|2003–04]] editions, and one championship for [[Étoile Sportive du Sahel|Étoile du Sahel]] in [[2018–19 Arab Club Champions Cup|2018–19]] and the same for [[Club Africain]] in [[1997 Arab Club Champions Cup|1997]]. As for the runners-up, the Tunisian teams took second place five times, twice for [[Espérance Sportive de Tunis|Espérance de Tunis]] in [[1986 Arab Club Champions Cup|1986]] and [[1995 Arab Club Champions Cup|1995]], twice also for [[Club Africain]] in [[1988 Arab Club Champions Cup|1988]] and [[2002 Arab Unified Club Championship|2002]], and [[Club Sportif Sfaxien|Club Sfaxien]] in [[2004–05 Arab Champions League|2004–05]].

Tunisian clubs have won the [[Arab Cup Winners' Cup]] three times. [[Stade Tunisien]] is the most titled Tunisian club in the championship with 2 titles in [[1989 Arab Cup Winners' Cup|1989]] and [[2001 Arab Cup Winners' Cup|2001]], followed by [[Club Africain]], which won the championship only once in [[1995 Arab Cup Winners' Cup|1995]]. As for [[Étoile Sportive du Sahel|Étoile du Sahel]], it was satisfied with the runner-up position in [[1995 Arab Cup Winners' Cup|1995]] in the match that brought it together with [[Club Africain]].
