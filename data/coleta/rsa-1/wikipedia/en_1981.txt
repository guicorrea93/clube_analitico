The '''National Soccer League 1981''' season was the fifth season of the Australian [[National Soccer League]] (NSL). The league was known as the '''Philips Soccer League''' ('''PSL''') in a sponsorship arrangement with Dutch electronics company [[Philips]]. The champions were [[Sydney City SC|Sydney Slickers]], winning their third title.

==Changes from 1980==
The league increased from 14 to 16 teams prior to the 1981 season. Despite finishing second last—ordinarily a relegation position—in 1980, [[West Adelaide SC|West Adelaide]] (known as Adelaide Hawks in 1981) were retained for 1981. Wooden-spooner [[St George FC|St George]] were the only team to be relegated, with three new teams being introduced - [[Preston Lions FC|Preston Makedonia]] (nicknamed Rams in 1981) [[Sydney Olympic FC|Sydney Olympic]] (Sydney Olympians in 1981) and [[Wollongong Wolves FC|Wollongong City]] (Wollongong Wolves).<ref>{{Cite news |url=http://www.newspapers.com/image/123990961/?terms=st%252Bgeorge%252Brelegated |title=Saints out of bigger PSL |last=Curran |first=Brian |date=1 November 1980 |work=The Sydney Morning Herald |access-date=2019-08-26 |language=en|url-access=subscription|page=70|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> 

Prior to the season commencing, in what was described as "Australianisation" of the national league, nicknames were imposed on the clubs, some of which were not popular with the teams themselves. [[Marconi Stallions FC|Marconi]] President, Tony Labbozzetta expressed his dislike of the enforced nickname Leopards, suggesting "Call us Datsun", referring to a naming rights deal the club had made with the Japanese carmaker.<ref>{{Cite news |url=http://www.newspapers.com/image/121332949/ |title=Hellas by any other name–No |last=Schwab |first=Laurie |date=16 February 1981 |work=The Age |access-date=2019-08-26 |page=31 |language=en |via=Newspapers.com|url-access=subscription}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |url=http://www.newspapers.com/image/122641517/ |title=Marconi seal $250,000 contract with car firm |last=Curran |first=Brian |date=29 January 1981 |work=The Sydney Morning Herald |access-date=2019-08-26 |language=en |via=Newspapers.com |page=32 |url-access=subscription}}</ref> Along with the new nicknames, new macho logos were introduced. John Clark, a marketing executive at the Australian Soccer Federation, claimed that "we are not trying to take ethnicism out of soccer" and that they were trying to appeal to boys "not following in their fathers' footsteps, boys now into space invaders, Buck Rogers in the 25th century, who aren't following soccer."<ref>{{Cite news |url=http://www.newspapers.com/image/121824683/ |title=Aussie image |last=Larkin |first=John |date=6 August 1981 |work=The Age |access-date=2019-08-26 |language=en|via=Newspapers.com|url-access=subscription|page=2}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Schwab |first=Laurie |date=28 January 1981 |title=Giants, Wolves, Gunners and a Packer look |work=Soccer Action |url=https://drive.google.com/file/d/0BxjATk9ezHsaZXNWN19LcllZdEU |url-access=subscription |page=3|access-date=2020-05-19}}</ref>

The league consisted of a double round-robin format, played between February and September. The league was required to finish by the end of September due to Australia's hosting of the [[1981 FIFA World Youth Championship]] in October.<ref>{{Cite news |url=http://www.newspapers.com/image/123991620/ |title=Bigger PSL will feel the squeeze |date=2 November 1980 |work=The Sydney Morning Herald |access-date=2019-08-26 |language=en|url-access=subscription|page=83|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> Unlike the 1980 season, a post-season finals series was not held and the league championship was awarded to the team at the top of the table. The final series was reintroduced the following year.<ref>{{Cite news |url=http://www.newspapers.com/image/123992125/ |title=Top-four scrapped in PSL shake-up |last=Curran |first=Brian |date=3 November 1980 |work=The Sydney Morning Herald |access-date=2019-08-26 |language=en|url-access=subscription|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |url=http://www.newspapers.com/image/121448625/ |title=Grand final in Philips League |date=21 December 1981 |work=The Age |access-date=2019-08-26 |language=en|page=19|url-access=subscription|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>
