List of Clubs

Note that this list includes all clubs mentioned in this document, including a number of foreign ones, but is not necessarily exhaustive.
The short versions of the names on the left hand side are used in this document; an additional indication of the home town is only included if a homonymous club from another town appears in the document as well.
The full names on the right hand side appeared at least once in contemporary publications (usually the "Nederlandsche Sport") but for most clubs other versions appeared as well: in particular, the words "voetbal" and "football" as well as "vereeniging" and "club" were often interchanged, sometimes apparently at random, and while nearly all early Dutch football clubs originated from an older cricket club, the "cricket" part was not always included in the name.
Additional information is most welcome.

All clubs in italics were members of the N.V.A.B. at least at some point during the season.

Administrative Developments

The general meeting of the federation held on Oct 18, 1891 at the American Hôtel in Amsterdam accepted a new Algemeen Reglement, which implied two significant changes.
First of all, the referee became solely responsible for all decisions and did not (as until then) have to consult the two umpires (one provided by each club); these were "relegated" to linesmen (grensrechters) whose main task became to determine whether the ball had crossed the line (and who had last touched it). Nine men were designated as official federation referees (bondsscheidsrechters): F. Ceret, H. Coops, J. de Goeje, H. Isbrücker, C. Jibben, Joh. Meĳer, J. Vaes, H. Westerveld and L. Wĳnands.
Secondly, the penalty kick, only recently introduced in Britain (the first was taken on Jun 6, 1891 in Airdrie), entered the rules, to be awarded in case of a serious offense (zwaar vergrĳp) within 12 yards of the goalline.
The same meeting also decided (by 14 votes against 2) that all six clubs in the Eerste Klasse had to play ten matches (so meet all others home and away).

At a board meeting on Nov 21, 1891, in Leiden, it was decided to use goal average (deeling) as tie-breaker in case of equality on points (or rather points/match ratio) as opposed to the goal difference rule en vigueur in the 1890/91 season.

In December 1891 Hollandia, based in Frankfurt am Main, Germany, joined the N.V.A.B.; in March 1892 they were followed by Thor, a football and athletics club in Roermond just founded by P.W. Calkoen (an athlete who also ran under the nom de plume "Ajamblanda" – the Malay translation of his family name); Thor were the first club from the southeastern province of Limburg to join the federation (and the only one to do so in the nineteenth century). However, both Hollandia and Thor resigned again within a year.

At the general meeting on May 8, 1892, in the "Zuid-Hollandsch Koffiehuis" in Den Haag, the federation was officially renamed: "Nederlandsche Voetbal- en Athletiek-Bond" (instead of "De Nederlandsche Voetbal- en Athletische Bond").

International Activity

Antwerp/Rotterdam match

After having played three times in Breda against Rotterdam club sides in 1890 (against Concordia in March and November and against Olympia in October), this season Antwerp F.C. travelled all the way to Rotterdam to take on the new club which had resulted from the merger of their two earlier opponents. As on each of the previous three occasions, the Britons travelled back to Belgium beaten. 

[Dec 20, 1891; Rotterdam]
R.C. en V.V.        5-0 Antwerp F.C.

Cancelled visit Barnes F.C.

A tour of Barnes F.C., who had agreed to play four matches in the first week of January, eventually did not materialise, the English calling the trip off three days before the first match, apparently because of frosty weather in the Netherlands. Similar visits by British sides to the Netherlands had been cancelled in previous years, usually a few weeks before the scheduled dates, provoking the following exasperated comment in the "Nederlandsche Sport": "In het voorbĳgaan zĳ echter gezegd, dat de wĳze, waarop nu reeds eenige jaren achtereen een loopje wordt genomen met den Bond, zeer on-Engelsch is, en het is waarlĳk niet te verwonderen, dat er weinigen meer worden gevonden, die in de komst van Engelsche voetballers gelooven, vóórdat zĳ ze zien."
