Administrative Developments

At the board meeting on Aug 4, 1894, eerste secretaris L.J. Wĳnands suggested to set up the "Derde Competitie" districtwise, so that new members such as Voorwaarts, who were based in Tiel, could enter without having to travel to Amsterdam or Rotterdam. He argued against a division by provinces (as used in the Tweede Klasse in 1890/91, which had been played in knock-out format). His suggestion was approved unanimously, also in view of the fact that several clubs from the eastern provinces had either joined the N.V.A.B. recently (at the time, Voorwaarts and Quick from Nĳmegen) or expressed an interest in doing so. R. Schut, the tweede secretaris, then proposed to let the various district champions meet each other for the overall title, but given the financial and logistical problems that might have entailed, it was decided to leave arranging such matches to the clubs in question, on a voluntary basis, with support from the N.V.A.B. if required; all district champions were to be awarded a silver medal.
At the same meeting a request of Haarlem to be admitted to Eerste Klasse was refused, as approving such a request was against the regulations and there were no grounds for it whatsoever ("er hoegenaamd geen reden voor is").

On Sep 30, 1894, Haarlem repeated their promotion petition at the annual general meeting, held at café "Rapp", Mariaplaats, in Utrecht, but predictably the vote went against them (3 in favour, 13 against, 10 abstentions).
Thus, Haarlem had to settle for their fifth successive season in the Tweede Klasse. They were the only club from the previous season to enter it, promoted Rapiditas being replaced by relegated city rivals Victoria while the Gooi-Hercules merger was dissolved (newly founded Hilversum club 't Gooi joined the N.V.A.B. at the start of the season and Utrecht-based Hercules remained members, but both sat out the season) and Quick, who had been a fixture of the Tweede Klasse in each of the previous three seasons in spite of only once completing their fixtures, withdrew. Replacing Quick were two other clubs from the capital, Olympia (who seamlessly continued the time-honoured Amsterdam tradition of absconding during the season) and U.n.I.; in addition Kralingen, the winners of the Derde Competitie in 1893/94, decided to join (note they were not promoted – any member club of the N.V.A.B. was entitled to enter the Tweede Klasse competition).

At the board meeting one week later, on Oct 6, 1894, in Amsterdam, the new set-up of the Derde Competitie was decided, with sixteen clubs divided over five districts (Z.A.C. of Zwolle were included in both the eastern district (a) and the northern district (c), in order to provide new members Frisia of Leeuwarden with some competitive matches).
Also during October, clubs from Den Haag and Amsterdam set up local federations, who started their own leagues at the end of that month; Rotterdam followed suit in November (the new R.V.B. league might in fact be seen as a continuation of the 1893/94 Derde Competitie).

On May 12, 1895, at the general meeting held at café "Rapp", Mariaplaats, in Utrecht, there was yet another vote on the promotion of Haarlem, who had finished runners-up in the Tweede Klasse for the second season in succession, but after some discussion this was again rejected (6 votes in favour, 19 against, 31 abstentions). Their three defeats during the season against Eerste Klasse opposition (H.V.V., H.F.C. and Rapiditas) had done nothing to strengthen their (legally inexistent) case.

Districtcompetities

Given the success of both the Derde Competitie and the Oostelĳke Competitie in the previous season, and the influx of new member clubs during the summer break, it was decided to play the "third competition" in districts. Note that, like in the 1893/94 season, this competition was explicitly not called "Derde Klasse". At a board meeting on Oct 6, 1894, five groups were agreed upon, as follows: 

a: Prinses Wilhelmina, Z.A.C. Zutphania, U.D.;
b: Vitesse, Get-on, Voorwaarts, Quick (Nĳmegen), Victoria (Den Bosch);
c: Z.A.C., Frisia;
d: Excelsior (Rotterdam), Celeritas (Rotterdam), Leonidas;
e: Batavia, Neptunus, L.V.V.

The winners of districts d and e were to meet over two legs to establish an
overall "western" winner; note that Z.A.C. entered both districts a and c.
During the season, district b became known as "Afdeeling 1", district e as "Afdeeling 2" and district d as "Afdeeling 3" (the rationale behind this particular permutation has been lost in time), whereas districts a and c (which overlapped and thus could not properly be described as "divisions") were referred to as "Oostelĳke competitie" and "Noordelĳke competitie" respectively. These are also the denominations used below. In spite of the apparent lack of uniformity in their names, all five competitions were of equal status (and in no way part of a league hierarchy).

Onderbonden

After the proposal by Louis van Rooy to let reserve sides of Eerste Klasse clubs enter the Tweede Klasse had been rejected at the start of the previous season, R.A.P. invited other Amsterdam football clubs (Quick, A.A.C., U.n.I., Volharding and Olympia) to a meeting on Sep 29, 1894, at which the foundation of a football federation for Amsterdam was approved; the A.V.B. (Amsterdamsche Voetbal Bond) was founded formally on Oct 17, 1894. Two weeks earlier, on Oct 1, 1894, clubs from Den Haag had already established the H.V.B. (Haagsche Voetbal Bond), and on the initiative of Sparta, Rotterdam followed suit: on Nov 21, 1894, the R.V.B. (Rotterdamsche Voetbal Bond) came into existence.
All three new city federations immediately launched a local league, although only one Eerste Klasse side (Sparta) made serious use of this new possibility for the reserve side to play competitive matches; H.V.V. withdrew their reserves from the H.V.B. League before they had played a match (however, at the end of the season H.V.V.'s first team challenged a selection of the H.V.B. clubs), and R.A.P.'s reserve side forfeited half its matches.

International Activity

Antwerp/Rotterdam matches

In June 1894 the "British Continentals" suggested the foundation of an "International Sporting Association"; those interested were to contact Mr. Fred. Manning, 76 Strand, London WC. In November 1894, that association reportedly had ten member clubs, most of them dominated by British expats: four in Paris (White Rovers, Standard A.C., Stade Français and Inter-Nos), four in Berlin (Berlin C.C., English F.C., Academic Sport Club and Excelsior), and one each in Copenhagen (K.B.) and in Karlsruhe (Karlsruhe Kickers, a short-lived split-off of the Karlsruher F.V. adorning itself with the epithet "Meistermannschaft des Continents" in true Baron von Münchhausen spirit and supposedly responsible for the later and somewhat – to the ears of native Dutch speakers – amusing abundance of football clubs named "Kickers" in southern Germany (e.g. Offenbach, Stuttgart, Würzburg) – Up the Frogs!).
Apparently none of the "British" sports clubs in Belgium had cared to join this "International Sporting Association" but three of them expressed interest in playing friendlies with clubs from Rotterdam. Victoria arranged five international matches: two meetings with both F.C. Liégeois (away on Jan 6 and at home on Feb 24) and Antwerp F.C. (away on Dec 9 and at home on Mar 17) as well as one with Brussels F.A. (on Feb 3 in Rotterdam), but for a variety of reasons, four of these five matches did not take place. Likewise, Sparta's intended home match against F.C. Liégeois on Jan 27 was cancelled, as was their match against Friesland A.C. from New York, which had been announced for Jan 13 in Rotterdam only to be called off by the visitors two days before the game.
However, both Sparta and Victoria (now a Tweede Klasse club) travelled to the Scheldestad and returned home victorious, thus resuming the tradition of Maasstad victories which had so rudely been interrupted in the 1893/94 season.
