Administrative Developments

On Sep 5, 1897, the N.V.B. board met in café "De Roode Leeuw", Amsterdam to discuss new regulations ("huishoudelĳk reglement van den N.V.B."), formulated by a commission installed by interim president Mulier. Apart from minor changes, these were approved; the main difference in the set-up of the league competitions was that from this season on, only the bottom finishers of the Eerste Klasse in each Afdeeling would have to enter a "Test Match", against the champions of the Tweede Klasse in their Afdeeling; if there were two Tweede Klasse groups, the two winners would first need to play off for the title.
At the same meeting, Engelberts proposed to readmit Go Ahead and Prinses Wilhelmina, both relegated after the Test Matches at the end of the previous season, to the Eerste Klasse in Afdeeling 2; this proposal was accepted.

Two weeks later, on Sep 19, 1897, an extraordinary general meeting was convened at the café "Rapp", Mariaplaats, in Utrecht, where a proposal to readmit Victoria (Rotterdam) to the Eerste Klasse (from which they had been relegated after losing their Test Match to city rivals Celeritas) was rejected (18 votes against, 5 in favour, 13 abstentions). This was the fourth time Victoria's membership of the Eerste Klasse had been put to the vote at a general meeting: on May 3, 1891, they had been voted in, as champions of the Tweede Klasse, in spite of not having been able to play a promotion match; on May 14, 1893, they had been reinstated at the top level, in spite of having lost all seven matches that season; and on May 6, 1894 they had been demoted after finishing bottom with two points from ten matches.
The same general meeting agreed to the new regulations, as accepted by the board two weeks earlier, after a few small amendments.

At a board meeting on Oct 10, 1897 in Amsterdam, the member clubs were divided over the various competitions, and it was stipulated that: "de kampioen van de 1e klas afd. I zal spelen op onzĳdig terrein tegen den kampioen 1e klas afd. II"; in other words, the two first class champions would play each other at a neutral venue to contest the national title ("landskampioenschap").

At a board meeting on Jan 15, 1898 in Den Haag, a new regulation was introduced in order to prevent clubs abandoning a match: any club leaving the field during the match would lose 0-5, to which all goals the club remaining on the field had already scored would be added. That board meeting also decided that the cup offered by H.M.C. Holdert would already been contested in the ongoing season, but weather conditions in February made that impossible.

Finally, at the general meeting concluding the season on May 15, 1898, at the Musis Sacrum, Velperplein, Arnhem, the regulations on matches determining promotion and relegation, a second class championship or the national championship, were changed. Instead of one match at a neutral venue, as in the season just finished, in the future two matches were to be played, both clubs involved hosting one, and in case both obtain two points, a third match at a neutral venue, possibly followed by 15 minutes of extra time.

Holdert-beker

In January 1898, H.M.C. "Hak" Holdert, former president of V.V.A. (Amsterdam), who had already offered a statue for the winners of a knock-out competition in the 1893/94 season, in which eventually only one match was played, offered a cup, to be contested as the English F.A. Cup ("volgens de Engelsche regeling").

At a board meeting on Jan 15, 1898 in Den Haag it was decided to stage a competition for this new trophy already in the ongoing 1897/98 season (after a vote among the six board members present; 3 voted in favour and 3 against, the tie resolved by the vote in favour of N.V.B. president Warner).
One week later, on Jan 22, the board agreed on the regulations. These included the possibility of a sudden death (or for those who dislike that term: golden goal) decision in case of a replay. The main points were:

  • the cup would remain the property of the N.V.B. irrespective of how often any club would win it;
  • the competition was to be open to all N.V.B. member clubs and all member clubs of officially recognised (local) federations;
  • matches would be held districtwise ("per afdeeling") until two clubs were left in all four districts; the remaining eight clubs would then enter a free draw for the quarter-final stage (and beyond);
  • in case of a draw, 15 minutes of extra time (in two periods of 7½ minutes each) were to be played and if necessary another 15 minutes; if a match was still drawn after that, it was to be replayed, with hosting right reversed; if the second match was drawn, sudden death extra time was to be played ("tot winnend doelpunt");
  • finally, semi-finals were to be played at neutral venues and the final at the field of R.A.P.; the latter stipulation was later changed, at the request of Holdert, to the final being held at a (neutral) venue to be determined by the N.V.B. board.

By the end of February a dozen clubs had registered for the new competition (Ajax, Be Quick, Haarlem, Hercules, H.F.C. H.V.V., Prinses Wilhelmina, Quick (Nĳmegen), R.A.P., Rapiditas, Victoria (Rotterdam) and Vitesse – Sparta (Rotterdam) had explicitly declined to enter, while four other Eerste Klasse clubs, viz. Celeritas, Go Ahead, H.B.S. and U.D., also failed to register), but at a meeting in Den Haag on Mar 5, the board unanimously decided to cancel the competition for the ongoing season after a prolonged stretch of bad weather in the first weeks of February had led to numerous match postponements in the western leagues.

Onderbonden

The Haarlem city federation was dissolved; local side Unitas unsuccessfully attempted to join the A.V.B. instead.
At the beginning of October, the Oostelĳke Voetbalbond was formed in Hengelo by three clubs (Olympia of Almelo, the reserve side of Prinses Wilhelmina, and local side Tubantia, who refrained from entering the Tweede Klasse in Afdeeling 2 of the N.V.B.), but it appears no regional league was contested. (Two years later, in November 1899, the Twentsche Voetbalbond was founded in Enschede, catering for clubs from the same region.)
The Eerste Klasse of the H.V.B. league was abandoned due to fixture congestion and replaced by a knock-out tournament.
Finally, as in the previous season, a regional competition was initiated by U.D. to provide their reserve side with some competitive action. Their three opponents were all former (and future) participants in N.V.B. competitions: Robur et Velocitas (Apeldoorn), S.I.O.D. (Kampen) and Z.A.C. (Zwolle).

International Activity

Antwerp/Rotterdam matches

While in the previous season only one match was played between clubs from the two harbour cities, this season none was played at all, although a match had been fixtured – the Belgians (and Britons) simply did not appear. 

[Jan 23, 1898; Rotterdam]
Sparta R.           n/p Antwerp F.C.        [Antwerp F.C. dns]
Visit "English Wanderers"

The visiting side consisted of players from various clubs and was assembled by Charles Craven from Maidstone, who had also been in charge of the tours of Maidstone F.C. in 1894 and 1895 and that of the English Wanderers in 1896 and 1897.

The tourists proved to be the weakest British side ever to have visited the Netherlands or indeed the continent (their overall record of seven losses from seven matches, two goals scored and 31 conceded is the worst among all overseas tours by British (and Irish) sides before World War II; they were even beaten by a Tweede Klasse selection), and as a result Mr. Craven asked for (and was granted) financial support from the N.V.B. to assemble better sides for future visits.
