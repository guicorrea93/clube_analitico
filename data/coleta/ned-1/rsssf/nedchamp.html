Netherlands - Champions

The traditional list of winners of the landskampioenschap started in 1898, as that was the first year a playoff between the winners of the western and eastern first classes was held. However, until 1896 the only first class section was in principle open to all clubs, not only western ones (and in fact, in the last three seasons before the introduction of the eastern section in 1896, an "eastern" club, Go Ahead of Wageningen, had played in it after having been promoted from the second class in 1893).

Not to consider the first class champions until 1896 as Dutch national champions is in fact less reasonable than discarding all Viennese champions until the Anschluß in 1938 from the list of Austrian champions or denying all Montevideo league winners until 1998 (!) the right to be called champions of Uruguay, given that no clubs from outside those two capital cities had any opportunity to compete for the "national" title during that period (with the single exception of the winners of the Styrian league in 1937, entitled to entering a promotion playoff for the 1937/38 season).
In fact, H.V.V. have a stronger claim to the title of national champions of the Netherlands for the 1890/91 season than Everton to that of national champions of England in the same season (or Preston North End in the two previous ones), given that, from its inception until 1986, admission to the Football League was decided upon in board rooms rather than on the field.

Indeed, one might argue that the first class winners up to 1896 had a greater claim to the title of champions of the Netherlands than the winners between 1897 and 1916, given that the 1916/17 season was the first (since the introduction of four separate, regional divisions for the 1896/97 season) in which clubs from all regions were eligible to play at the top level, following the establishment of the northern first class (or rather the elevation of the northern second class to first class status).

However, note that the so-called "unofficial champions" of 1888/89 (Concordia of Rotterdam) and 1889/90 (H.F.C.) were nothing of the sort, as there was no league and clubs just played each other as they saw fit. There is a much stronger (but nevertheless weak) case for considering H.F.C. and V.V.A. (unofficial) joint champions in the 1887/88 season (and likewise for R.A.P. and Vitesse to be listed as joint champions for 1896/97).
