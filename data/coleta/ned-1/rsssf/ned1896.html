Administrative Developments

At an extraordinary general meeting on Sep 1, 1895 at the Hôtel "l'Europe", Vreeburg, Utrecht, several changes in the regulations were approved, most importantly about the formation of a Dagelĳksch Bestuur (executive committee), to consist of five members, appointed by the existing Bestuur (board, henceforth also referred to as Algemeen Bestuur), to deal with day-to-day affairs.
After that, a letter from one of the member clubs, A.A.C., complaining about the lack of athletics events financed by the federation and threatening to leave the N.V.A.B., was discussed. As a result, J.C. Schröder formulated the following proposal, to be decided upon at the ordinary general meeting four weeks later: "De Buitengewone Algemeene Vergadering van den Nederlandsche Voetbal en Athletiek Bond, vergaderd te Utrecht, den 1sten September 1895, spreekt als hare gevoelens uit, dat het noodzakelĳk is, voortaan den Nederlandsche Voetbal en Athletiek Bond alleen te noemen Nederlandsche Voetbal Bond, dus eene radicale scheiding in het leven te roepen tusschen Voetbal en Athletiek."
That motion was accepted (by 23 votes to 6) at the general meeting on Sep 29, 1895 at café "de Vriendschap", Keistraat, Utrecht. Therefore, after this meeting the federation is officially called N.V.B. (Nederlandsche Voetbal Bond).

At the general meeting concluding the season, on May 17, 1896, at café "Rapp", Mariaplaats, Utrecht, a motion of censure was tabled by Haarlem against the N.V.B. board because of the way the conclusion of the Tweede Klasse competition had been handled by the Algemeen Bestuur three weeks earlier; after prolonged discussions this motion was rejected overwhelmingly (36 votes against, 2 in favour, 15 abstentions). Possibly Haarlem's persistent pressure to gain admittance to the Eerste Klasse in previous seasons in spite of not fulfilling the requirements had earned them little sympathy – in this case, at least, they had valid grounds for complaint, the board having hidden behind a supposed confusion between the intentions of the Dagelĳksch Bestuur and the Algemeen Bestuur to replace the (unfair and badly formulated) rules by an ad hoc arrangement. (Note that all five members of the Dagelĳksch Bestuur also belonged to the Algemeen Bestuur.)
It certainly did not help Haarlem's cause that both team captain Smit and club secretary Gerth van Wĳk had openly admitted, in letters published in the "Nederlandsche Sport" on Apr 18, 1896, that Haarlem had wilfully distorted the competition, by stating, to illustrate the fact that Haarlem had been aware that the group matches would not count for the final stage: "Hierop bouwende, heb ik niet de minste moeite gedaan voor die wedstrĳden tegen U.N.I. volledige elftallen in het veld te brengen" (Smit) and "daarom deed zĳ, toen zĳ van hare plaats voor de beslissingsronde verzekerd was, niet de minste moeite U.N.I. te verslaan" (Gerth van Wĳk) – that must have left a sour taste at Volharding, who would have qualified for the final stage had Haarlem won its last two group matches against U.n.I.
After that, several proposals were discussed to adapt the procedures to obtain membership of the Eerste and Tweede Klasse, as the existing regulations would in time lead to a steady increase. Wurfbain suggested to have the top two teams of the Tweede Klasse meet the bottom two of the Eerste Klasse, with only the winners of such test matches entering the Eerste Klasse in the next season. Van Ĳzeren proposed to introduce admission matches for the Tweede Klasse, with candidate clubs having to challenge an existing member club, which collected at least three points during the season, and only gaining entry in case of a win or two draws against that club.
While the proposal of Van Ĳzeren was rejected, discussion of the motion by Wurfbain was deferred until after Aug 1, 1896, when a commission, formed to formulate a new competition set-up, was to have made suggestions.

Tweede Klasse

The nine clubs who registered for the Tweede Klasse were divided over two groups,
a northern one with the four clubs from Amersfoort, Amsterdam and Haarlem, and a
southern one with the five clubs from Den Haag and Rotterdam; the top two of both
groups qualified for a final group in which clubs were to meet those of the other
group twice, each thus playing four matches.

Onderbonden

In addition to the three local federations formed in the previous season (Amsterdam, Den Haag and Rotterdam), a new city federation was formed in December 1895 in Haarlem, the fourth major footballing centre in Holland, by five local clubs: Haarlem, H.F.C., Ripperda, Sophiadal and Unitas.
One month earlier, the formation of a local federation had been discussed in Arnhem as well, but that appears to have led to nothing. In contrast, three clubs from the northern province of Frisia played out a regional league, albeit apparently without establishing a formal federation.
At the beginning of the season, the Haagsche Voetbal Bond consulted the N.V.B. about the inclusion of clubs from the neighbouring towns of Delft (Concordia) and Leiden (Ajax). The national federation disapproved of the idea, but the H.V.B. ignored that and went ahead regardless, in spite of the fact that Delft had no suitable ground and thus Concordia had to play all matches away.
Partially because of those additional entries, the H.V.B. introduced a Tweede Klasse, as did the A.V.B. in Amsterdam. In contrast, the R.V.B. league was contested by three teams only, two of them also active in the southern section of the national second class.

Arnhem

On the initiative of Vitesse, the formation of an Arnhemsche Voetbalbond was
discussed in November 1895; it was to have two leagues, Vitesse II, Olympia,
Gelria and De Pĳl entering the Eerste Klasse, and Vitesse III, Olympia II
and Arnhemia the Tweede Klasse, but nothing appears to have come of that.

Antwerp/Rotterdam matches

As in the 1894/95 season, there were two encounters between club sides from the two port cities. In the previous season, both Victoria and Sparta had travelled to Belgium, this year Victoria and Antwerp exchanged visits. The return match in Antwerp, played in bad weather, was abandoned by the referee in the second half, to the bemusement of the visitors. 

[Oct 20, 1895; Rotterdam]
Victoria R.         2-4 Antwerp F.C.        

[Dec 15, 1895; Antwerpen]
Antwerp F.C.        abd Victoria R.         [abandoned at 2-0 in second half]
