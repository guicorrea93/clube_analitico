List of Clubs

Note that this list includes all clubs mentioned in this document (apart from a few referred to in connection with later events), including a number of foreign ones, but is not necessarily exhaustive.
The short versions of the names on the left hand side are used in this document; an additional indication of the home town is only included if a homonymous club from another town appears in the document as well.
The full names on the right hand side appeared at least once in contemporary publications but for most clubs other versions appeared as well: in particular, the words "voetbal" and "football" as well as "vereeniging" and "club" were often interchanged, sometimes apparently at random, and while nearly all early Dutch football clubs originated from an older cricket club, the "cricket" part was not always included in the name.
Additional information is most welcome.

Administrative Developments

The season started on a bad note when the annual Algemeene Vergadering at Musis Sacrum, Velperplein, in Arnhem, on Sep 3, 1899, had to be cancelled because only twelve clubs had sent representatives, whereas the regulations required one quarter of the membership (56 clubs at the time) to be represented. Only two of the thirteen Eerste Klasse clubs (newly promoted Ajax from Leiden and local side Vitesse) had bothered to attend. The meeting was eventually held three weeks later at the same venue.
By the time of that second meeting, there had been a somewhat surprising addition to the membership, as S.O.K. from Medan (on Sumatra) had joined, apparently on the instigation of Willem Mulier, the first ever N.V.A.B. president, who had recently become the editor of the "Deli Courant", a local newspaper.
In contrast, long-standing members Victoria from Rotterdam resigned in October. Victoria had joined the federation in 1890 and had since become the first Dutch "yoyo" team: they had been promoted to the Eerste Klasse in 1891, were reprieved from relegation in 1893 in spite of losing all their league matches but failed to obtain re-election in 1894, immediately bounced back as Tweede Klasse champions in 1895 but went down again in 1897 and had finished last in their Tweede Klasse group in the previous season.

In January 1900 two new regional federations, those of Noord-Brabant and Zeeland, petitioned for official recognition from the N.V.B. and were granted that during the season. Meanwhile, the dominance of new entrants Velocitas in the Tweede Klasse of the southern Afdeeling 4 triggered a suggestion to include the southern champions in the promotion matches for the Eerste Klasse of Afdeeling 1; no action was taken on this, but after the cadets, who represented the Koninklĳke Militaire Academie (Royal Military Academy) in Breda, had won the Holdert-beker, the domestic cup tournament, they were eventually admitted to the Eerste Klasse of Afdeeling 1 for the 1900/01 season (by a board decision on Sep 15, 1900, which also promoted U.D.), in spite of opposition from Amsterdam and Haarlem, whose clubs did not relish the journey to Breda.

When it appeared increasingly likely that H.V.V. would win the championship and Velocitas the cup, an article in the Mar 3, 1900 issue of the "Nederlandsche Sport", presumably written by W. Coops, suggested to stage a "Super Cup" match between the two: "Mocht de H.V.V. dan het kampioenschap, Velocitas den beker gewonnen hebben, eene eventueele ontmoeting zou dan zeer belangwekkend zĳn, eene waardige apotheose aan het eind van een welbesteed seizoen. Als er tenminste tĳd voor gevonden zal kunnen worden, wat wel te hopen, maar lang niet zeker is." The author was decades ahead of his time: the first Dutch "Super Cup" match would be played nearly half a century later (in 1949, between S.V.V. and Quick (Nĳmegen), as a one-off; it would still take four more decades for the tournament to be established as a season opener, first in 1991), while the first edition of the English Charity Shield featuring the reigning league champions and F.A. cup winners was that of 1921, between champions Burnley and cup holders Tottenham Hotspur.

The season was closed by a general meeting on Jul 1, 1900 in Utrecht at "het Haagsche Koffiehuis", where the main item of discussion was the future organisation of the federation – in particular an extensive proposal by H.B.S. president Stokvis to decentralise it. This eventually was firmly rejected (11 votes in favour, 25 against).
Other items on the agenda were not discussed due to the duration of the above debate; these included changes to the regulations, suggested by the N.V.B. board, one stipulating that new members would in principle belong to the Derde Klasse (instead of the Tweede Klasse) and another introducing a "golden goal" rule for deciding matches at neutral venues ("Met dien verstande, dat de wedstrĳd op onzĳdig terrein een beslissing brengen moet en dus doorgespeeld zal worden tot een der partĳen een punt behaald heeft"). Another new regulation, warmly supported by the N.V.B. board, had been suggested by H.V.V. and R.A.P., both miffed after losing out on gate receipts because of the no shows of Sparta and H.F.C. towards the end of the season: "Komt een vereeniging meer dan tweemaal in hetzelfde seizoen niet op, dan wordt ze onherroepelĳk aan het begin van het volgende in een lagere competitie ingedeeld."
The relevant changes were eventually accepted at the continuation of the meeting on Sep 9, 1900 at the same venue.

International Activity

Although at the beginning of the season another visit of the "English Wanderers" side was discussed, and Southampton F.C. had planned a tour to the Netherlands around Christmas, eventually no English football team crossed the Channel. This may partially have been due to the ill-feeling towards Britain in the Netherlands caused by the Second Boer War, which had started in October 1899; Dutch public opinion was firmly on the side of the Boer Republics Transvaal and Oranje Vrĳstaat.
In October, Katendrecht intended to host the "Dresden Footballclub" (it is not certain to which of the existing football clubs in Dresden this referred, possibly the Neuer Dresdner F.C. founded in 1893) but that never happened; in January, H.V.V. received an invitation from Slavia to visit them in Prague but the negotiations on that did not lead to a match either. Instead there were a few low profile visits to the Netherlands by foreign clubs before the undisputed highlight of the international calendar this season, the first ever major international club tournament, held in Brussels during the Easter holidays in 1900.

First, early in the season, the Duisburger F.V. travelled to Arnhem to play Vitesse (whom they had already played home and away at the beginning of the year); the net profits of that match, like those of various league matches at the time, such as those between Ajax and Haarlem and Sparta and R.A.P., both played two weeks earlier, and that between D.F.C. and Concordia, played two weeks later, were dedicated to support the Boer cause in Transvaal.
