Saturday Football 

In the beginning of the fifties, Dutch football was in crisis.  The
K.N.V.B. refused to allow professional football, so the best players
went abroad.  These were not eligible for the national side anymore,
and between November 1949 and May 1954, the Dutch team lost 22 of 28
matches played.
The top level being divided over six "first classes" meant the pool
of good players was spread over (too) many clubs, leading to a low
average level of play.
Starting with this season, an attempt was made to improve on this
situation by reducing the number of first classes to initially five
and later four groups, while relaxing the regional division of the
top level clubs.
However, there was no significant improvement in the level of play
until the introduction of semi-professional football in 1954.
