The '''Nigeria Premier Football League''' ('''NPFL''') also known as the '''EuroMatch Premier Football League''' for sponsorship reasons<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/08/26/7-5m-boost-euromatch-signs-3-year-deal-to-become-npfl-title-sponsor/|title=$7.5 million dollars boost: Euromatch signs 3-year deal to become NFL title sponsor|date=26 August 2026|newspaper=[[This Day]]}}</ref> and (formerly the '''Nigeria Professional Football League''') is the highest level of club football in [[Nigerian football league system]]. It is organized by the Nigeria Premier Football League (NPFL) Board under the authority of the [[Nigeria Football Federation]]. It has been organised since 1972. It is fed into by the [[Nigeria National League]] (NNL).

The NPFL runs from August to May, with each of the 20 teams playing 38 matches (facing every other team twice, once at home and once away), totaling 380 matches per season. Most games are scheduled for Sunday afternoons, while others are played on Saturday or weekday evenings.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://npfl.com.ng/2023/12/11/npfl-moves-to-bring-back-friday-night-matches/|title=NPFL moves to bring back Friday night matches|work=npfl.com.ng|date=11 December 2023|accessdate=7 September 2026}}</ref>

The current champions are [[Enugu Rangers]], who won their 9th title in the 2025–26 season.

==Name history==
The league was previously known as the Nigeria Premier League between 1993 and 2000, and between 2003 and 2013.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://dailypost.ng/2023/01/07/npfl-new-name-for-nigerian-league-as-akwa-united-insurance-clash-in-season-opener/|location=Lagos, Nigeria|title=NPFL:new name for Nigerian league|date=8 January 2023|access-date=11 June 2023|archive-date=11 June 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230611194209/https://dailypost.ng/2023/01/07/npfl-new-name-for-nigerian-league-as-akwa-united-insurance-clash-in-season-opener/|url-status=live|newspaper=[[Daily Post (Nigeria)|Daily Post]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|url=https://dailypost.ng/2023/09/10/nff-approves-npfl-name-change/|location=Lagos, Nigeria|title=NFF approves NPFL name change|date=10 September 2023|access-date=12 September 2023|archive-date=11 September 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230911150911/https://dailypost.ng/2023/09/10/nff-approves-npfl-name-change/|url-status=live|newspaper=[[Daily Post (Nigeria)|Daily Post]]}}</ref>

It has also been known as the "Nigerian Premiership" (2000–2003); "Nigerian Professional League" (1990–1993); Nigerian National League - First Division (1979–1990) and "the (Nigerian) National League" or "Nigerian Football League" (1972–1979) and more recently "Nigeria Professional Football League" (2014–2023).

==International partnerships==
On 27 April 2016, The [[Liga de Fútbol Profesional|Liga Nacional de Fútbol Profesional]], organisers of the [[La Liga]] sealed a five-year [[memorandum of understanding]] deal with the NPFL on capacity building and idea exchange signed by the League Management Company chairman, [[Shehu Dikko]] and the [[La Liga]] President [[Javier Tebas]]. The NPFL and [[La Liga]] partnership has already started bearing fruit as during the January transfer window, [[Super Eagles]] fringe player [[Ezekiel Bassey]] was signed on loan from [[Enyimba]] to [[Barcelona B]] on a six-month deal with an option of 3-years come end of the season based on performance. The NPFL AllStars (made up of the league's best players) have also been playing Pre-Season as well as Winter break friendlies with top flight Spanish sides like [[Atlético Madrid]], [[Málaga CF|Málaga]], [[Valencia CF|Valencia]], [[Villarreal CF|Villarreal]], etc. In an effort to expose and test local players against top players and sides.

===2019 season===
The league consisted of 24 teams. Four were promoted from the [[Nigeria National League]]. Twenty teams remained from the previous season which was not completed. They were divided into two groups of 12 teams. The top three teams in each group contested for the title in the NPFL Super six championship playoff in [[Lagos]]. The People's Elephant finished at the top of the log with 12 points from five games.

[[Enyimba F.C.|Enyimba]] vs [[Kano Pillars F.C.|Kano Pillars]] is considered the biggest match of the season because of their rivalry and they remain the 2 teams yet to finish outside the top eight for 12 consecutive seasons.

== Management ==
The League Management Company (shortly and commonly known as the 'LMC') is the legal [[association football]] League [[Sports governing body|governing body]] of the Nigeria Professional Football League. It was created and incorporated by the [[Nigeria Football Federation]] in 2012 to take over the nearly-collapsed Nigeria Football League (NFL), the former league governing body.<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.goal.com/en-ng/news/4093/nigerian-football/2012/11/29/3566201/nigeria-football-federation-gives-nigeria-premier-league |title= NFF gives Nigeria Premier League green light on its AGM |work= [[Goal.com]] |date= 29 November 2012 |access-date= 10 August 2020 |archive-date= 17 October 2020 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20201017044223/https://www.goal.com/en-ng/news/4093/nigerian-football/2012/11/29/3566201/nigeria-football-federation-gives-nigeria-premier-league |url-status= live }}</ref>

===History===
The NFF created the Nigeria Premier League, organized by the Nigeria Football League in 1990 as a step in attaining [[Professionalism in association football|full professionalism]] as the sole regulatory for [[football in Nigeria]]. At the [[Onikan Stadium]] on 12 May 1990, the league was given a name as it was then known as the 'Professional League'.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.yeswefoot.com/history-of-nigeria-premier-league/|title=History of the Nigerian Premier League|last=Segun|first=Solomon|website=yeswefoot.com|date=26 April 2014|access-date=10 August 2020|archive-date=19 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200919120952/http://www.yeswefoot.com/history-of-nigeria-premier-league/|url-status=live}}</ref>

However, in November 2012 with the agreement and support of the [[National Sports Commission]], [[Nigeria]]'s sports regulatory authority, the NFF constituted an Interim Management Committee (IMC) for the League as part of measures to avert a total collapse of the top tier professional League following the downfall of the Nigeria Football League (NFL) which arose from difficult legal and administrative impediments.<ref>{{Cite news|url= https://www.vanguardngr.com/2016/10/kudos-league-management-company-lmc/|location=Lagos, Nigeria|title= Kudos to the League Management Company|date= 7 October 2016|access-date= 11 October 2020|archive-date= 15 October 2020|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20201015115309/https://www.vanguardngr.com/2016/10/kudos-league-management-company-lmc/|url-status= live|newspaper=[[Vanguard (Nigeria)|Vanguard]]}}</ref> The IMC supervised the formation and incorporation of the LMC to run a transparent and commercially viable professional league.

=== Achievements ===
The first step the LMC took was to rebrand the League name, from the Nigerian Premier League to the Nigeria Professional Football League (NPFL), or simply the Professional 'Football' League)<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://npfl.ng/index.php/league-management-company-lmc |title=About League Management Company |access-date=10 August 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170606045717/http://npfl.ng/index.php/league-management-company-lmc |archive-date=6 June 2017 |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://npfl.ng/index.php/about-npfl/league-management-company-lmc/faqs |title=Faqs |access-date=10 August 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170531223815/http://www.npfl.ng/index.php/about-npfl/league-management-company-lmc/faqs |archive-date=31 May 2017 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Also, the LMC signed a [[Dollar|$]]34m TV rights deal to broadcast league matches which lasted until 2017.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://venturesafrica.com/nigeria-football-league-gets-34m-tv-rights-deal/|title=Nigeria Football League gets 34 million dollars TV rights deal|work=venturesafrica.com|date=20 August 2013|access-date=10 August 2020|archive-date=14 September 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190914052933/http://venturesafrica.com/nigeria-football-league-gets-34m-tv-rights-deal/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|url=https://nationaldailyng.com/super-sports-pulled-out-partnership-with-npfl/|location=Lagos, Nigeria|title=Supersports pulls out of partnership with NPFL|date=24 April 2017|access-date=11 October 2020|archive-date=27 March 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230327233605/https://nationaldailyng.com/super-sports-pulled-out-partnership-with-npfl/|url-status=live|newspaper=National Daily}}</ref>

In 2015, the League Management Company solicited financial support from the Government in order to upgrade existing grounds, provision of required broadcast and medical equipment and facilities in the stadiums.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.goal.com/en-ng/news/4111/npfl/2015/08/22/14649642/shehu-dikko-appeals-to-governors-for-stadia-facilities|title=Shehu Dikko appeals to governors for stadia facilities upgrade|last=Ahmadu|first=Samuel|date=22 August 2015|website=Goal|access-date=1 June 2019|archive-date=1 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190601193339/https://www.goal.com/en-ng/news/4111/npfl/2015/08/22/14649642/shehu-dikko-appeals-to-governors-for-stadia-facilities|url-status=live}}</ref>

The League Management Company has its framework and rules governing the 20 clubs in the Nigeria Professional Football League each season.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://npfl.ng/lmc/|title=About the LMC|work=npfl.ng|access-date=10 August 2020|archive-date=16 April 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200416201632/https://npfl.ng/lmc/|url-status=live}}</ref> On 5 July 2016, the NPFL adopted the [[FIFA Transfer Matching System|TMS Domestic Transfer Matching System]] (DTMS), becoming the first league in [[Africa]] to do so.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.fifa.com/who-we-are/news/nigeria-football-federation-adopts-fifa-tms-domestic-transfer-matching-2811049|title=Nigeria Football Federation adopts FIFA TMS domestic transfer matching system|work=[[FIFA.com]]|date=5 July 2016|access-date=10 August 2020|archive-date=1 January 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210101135713/https://www.fifa.com/who-we-are/news/nigeria-football-federation-adopts-fifa-tms-domestic-transfer-matching-2811049|url-status=live}}</ref>

The League Management Company is currently headed by [[Shehu Dikko]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.goal.com/en-ng/news/4111/npfl/2014/12/27/7439252/shehu-dikko-is-new-league-management-company-boss|title=Shehu Dikko is the new League Management Company boss|work=[[Goal.com]]|access-date=10 August 2020|archive-date=30 April 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190430210100/https://www.goal.com/en-ng/news/4111/npfl/2014/12/27/7439252/shehu-dikko-is-new-league-management-company-boss|url-status=live}}</ref>
