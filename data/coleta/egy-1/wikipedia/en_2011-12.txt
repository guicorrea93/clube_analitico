The '''2011–12 Egyptian Premier League''' was the fifty-fifth season of the [[Egyptian Premier League]] since its establishment in 1948. The season began on 14 October 2011, with a total of 19 teams contesting the league.  [[Al-Ahly S.C.|Al Ahly]] won the last seven league titles.

This season, the league increased from 16 to 19 teams due to no relegation in the [[2010–11 Egyptian Premier League|2010–11]] season as a result of the [[2011 Egyptian revolution]].  Three teams were promoted from the [[Egyptian Second Division|second division]].

Following the [[Port Said Stadium disaster]] on 1 February 2012, the season was suspended.<ref>{{cite news|author=Abdel-Rahman Hussein|title=Egypt football match violence: dozens dead and hundreds injured|work=The Guardian|location=UK|date=2 February 2012|url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2012/feb/01/egypt-football-match-violence-dead}}</ref> At that time, teams had played between 14 and 17 games out of 30. [[Haras El-Hodood Club|Haras El-Hodood]] was in first place with a 12–1–1 record.  This result was considered a significant surprise by ''[[Al Ahram]]''.<ref name=want>{{cite news|title=Harras El-Hodoud want Champions League clarification|author=Mahmoud Elassal|date=11 March 2012|work=Ahram Online|url=http://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContent/6/51/36462/Sports/Egyptian-Football/Harras-ElHodoud-want-Champions-League-clarificatio.aspx|access-date=11 March 2012}}</ref> On 10 March 2012, a decision was reached to cancel the remainder of the season.

== Teams ==
[[Union Alexandria|Al Ittihad Al Sakandary]], [[Smouha Sporting Club|Smouha]] and [[Arab Contractors SC|Al Mokawloon Al Arab]] were the worst three teams in [[2010–11 Egyptian Premier League|2010–11]] but were not relegated to the 2011–12 [[Egyptian Second Division]].  Three teams were promoted from the [[2010–11 Egyptian Second Division]] – [[El Dakhleya]], [[Ghazl El-Mehalla|Ghazl El Mahalla]], and [[Telephonat Bani Sweif]] – bringing the league up to 19 teams total.

==Cancellation==
On 10 March 2012, a decision was reached to cancel the remainder of the season.<ref name=BBC>{{cite news|title=Egypt's Premier League cancelled|publisher=BBC|date=10 March 2012|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/17324997|access-date=11 March 2012}}</ref> A spokesperson for the [[Egyptian Football Association]] said the decision was made because there was insufficient time to play the remaining games before the [[Egypt national football team|national team]] was scheduled to compete in the [[Football at the 2012 Summer Olympics|2012 Olympics]] and qualifiers for the [[2013 Africa Cup of Nations]].<ref name=BBC /> [[Farouk Ga’afar]], coach of [[Tala'ea El-Gaish SC|El-Gaish]] strongly criticized the decision saying the decision would cost the country a lot of money and that "people sitting in coffee shops could make a better decision."<ref>{{cite news|title=El-Geish coach condemns EFA for league cancellation|date=11 March 2012|work=Ahram Online|url=http://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContent/6/51/36491/Sports/Egyptian-Football/ElGeish-coach-condemns-EFA-for-league-cancellation.aspx|access-date=11 March 2012}}</ref> It was the fifth time that Egypt has cancelled a season of football; previously 1954–55, 1970–71, 1973–74, and 1989–90 were cancelled for a variety of reasons.<ref name=BBC />

At the same time, it was announced that 18 teams would compete in a friendly tournament "Martyrs Cup" to raise money for families of those killed in Port Said incident.<ref name=BBC /> [[Al-Masry Club|Al Masry]], the home team whose fans rioted in Port Said, leaving 72 people dead and 150 injured, was barred from the tournament.<ref name=BBC /> Al Ahly, the visiting team during the attack, has been invited but has not confirmed whether they will participate or not.<ref name=Way /> The tournament is scheduled to commence on 29 March with [[Round-robin tournament|round robin]] play.  The league will be divided into two groups, and the games will take place in empty stadiums owned by the Egyptian Army.<ref name=Way>{{cite news|title=Egyptian Premier League season cancelled|date=11 March 2012|work=Soccerway|url=https://int.soccerway.com/news/2012/March/11/egyptian-premier-league-season-cancelled/|access-date=11 March 2012}}{{dead link|date=November 2026|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref><ref name=Super /> The top two teams from each group will advance to the semi-finals for a single elimination event.<ref name=Way /> The final is scheduled for 18 May.<ref name=BBC /> The [[Egypt Cup]] is expected to take place as normal, with Al Masry barred from competition.<ref name=Super>{{cite news|title=Egyptian PL cancelled after deadly violence |work=SuperSport|date=11 March 2012|url=http://www.supersport.com/football/africa/news/120311/Egyptian_PL_cancelled_after_deadly_violence|access-date=11 March 2012}}</ref>

Under normal circumstances, the league's top two teams get to participate in the [[African Champions League]]. It was not immediately clear if league leaders El-Hodood and Al-Ahly would be declared as the league's top two teams and invited to participate in the Champions League.<ref name=want />  "I am not against the league cancellation but I want to know: Will we be considered as the league champions?" asked El-Hodoud coach [[Tarek El-Ashry]].<ref name=want />
In the end, Al-Ahly and [[Zamalek SC|Zamalek]], who were the 2010-11 League Champions and runners-up, were selected to represent Egypt.  Al-Ahly went on to win the tournament.
