The '''Egyptian Premier League''' ({{langx|arz|الدوري المصري الممتاز}}), commonly known as the '''El Dawry''' ({{langx|arz|الدوري}}) and officially known as the '''ORA League''' ({{langx|arz|دوري أورا}}) for sponsorship reasons, is a professional [[association football]] league in [[Egypt]] and the highest level of the [[Egyptian football league system]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=الموقع الرسمي لرابطة الأندية المحترفة المصرية |url=https://egyptianproleague.com/league |access-date=2026-06-20 |website=egyptianproleague.com |language=en}}</ref> The league comprises 20 teams and operates on a system of [[promotion and relegation]] with the [[Egyptian Second Division A]]. Seasons mostly run from August to May. Unlike most other leagues, games are played on all days of the week.

The Egyptian Premier League was founded in 1948, unifying the local leagues that existed previously. [[List of Egyptian Premier League clubs|70 clubs]] have competed in the league since its founding. [[Al Ahly SC|Al Ahly]] have won the title 45 times, more than any other club. Their closest rivals, [[Zamalek SC|Zamalek]], have won the league 15 times. Only five other clubs have won the league; those clubs are [[Ghazl El Mahalla SC|Ghazl El Mahalla]], [[Ismaily SC|Ismaily]], [[Al Mokawloon Al Arab SC|Al Mokawloon Al Arab]], [[Olympic Club (Egypt)|Olympic Club]], and [[Tersana SC|Tersana]].

The Egyptian Premier League is one of the top national leagues, ranked First in [[Africa]] according to [[Confederation of African Football|CAF]]'s [[CAF 5-year ranking|5-year ranking]] for the 2024–25 season, based on performances in African competitions over the past five seasons. Egyptian teams have won the [[CAF Champions League]] a record 18 times, and Al Ahly was named the African Club of the Century by CAF.<ref>{{cite encyclopedia|url=https://www.britannica.com/topic/Al-Ahly|title=Al-Ahly|encyclopedia=[[Encyclopædia Britannica Online]]|access-date=16 June 2022}}</ref> Two clubs have also won the [[CAF Confederation Cup]].

The Egyptian Premier League once had among highest average stadium attendance in Africa and the [[Middle East]] until the [[Port Said Stadium riot]] occurred on 1 February 2012 after a league match involving [[Al Masry SC|Al Masry]] and [[Al Ahly SC|Al Ahly]], where 74 people were killed and more than 500 were injured.<ref name = "jubilation">{{cite web |author=James Legge |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/africa/in-pictures-jubilation-in-cairo-riots-in-port-said-8468133.html?action=gallery&ino=4 |title=In pictures: Jubilation in Cairo, riots in Port Said |work=[[The Independent]] |year=2013 |access-date=16 Jun 2022 |archive-date=2 December 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171202052549/https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/africa/in-pictures-jubilation-in-cairo-riots-in-port-said-8468133.html?action=gallery&ino=4 |url-status=live }}</ref> Since that date, all domestic football matches were played [[Behind closed doors (sport)|behind closed doors]] until 2017, when the local security authorities started to allow fans to attend selected matches with gradually increasing numbers starting from 100 attendance only and in 2021, the league started to welcome back thousands of supporters.

==History==
[[Association football]] was introduced to Egypt while it was [[History of Egypt under the British|occupied]] by the British. The first football club in Egypt was [[El Sekka El Hadid SC|El Sekka El Hadid]], which was founded in 1903. The [[Sultan Hussein Cup]] was founded in 1917, and though it was dominated by English clubs in its first years, until [[Zamalek SC|Zamalek]] won it for the first time in [[1921 Sultan Hussein Cup final|1921]], Egyptian clubs quickly gained power.<ref name="life">{{cite web |author=Mohamed El-Sayed |url=http://weekly.ahram.org.eg/2004/677/sp5.htm |title=When life began |work=Ahram Weekly |year=2004 |access-date=28 May 2021 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121021214801/http://weekly.ahram.org.eg/2004/677/sp5.htm |archive-date=21 October 2012}}</ref> The [[Egypt Cup]], which no British teams competed in, began in 1922, and won by Zamalek.<ref>{{cite web |author=Tarek Said |url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/egy22.html |title=Egypt 1921/22 |work=RSSSF |year=2019 |access-date=28 May 2021 |archive-date=9 February 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230209100644/https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/egy22.html |url-status=live }}</ref>
 
The first major football league in Egypt also began play in 1922; consisting of clubs from [[Cairo]], it was called the [[Cairo League]]. Three other leagues, in [[Alexandria]], on the [[Suez Canal]], and an obscure league in [[Bahary]]{{efn|Bahary is an area of Alexandria, but it is not clear whether the league represented that area or not}} began soon afterwards. It was at this time that the clubs [[Zamalek SC|Zamalek]] and [[Al-Ahly SC|Al-Ahly]]{{efn|first named El-Mokhtalat and then King Farouk Club}} began their dominance, with the two clubs regularly winning the Cairo League and the Egypt Cup.<ref name="list">{{cite web |author=Amro Hassanin and Tarek Said |url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/egychamp.html |title=Egypt - List of Champions |work=RSSSF |year=2020 |access-date=27 May 2021 |archive-date=24 July 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220724163938/https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/egychamp.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

In 1938, the [[Egyptian Football Association]] (EFA) took control of the Cairo Zone Competition, along with the other three leagues. The Cairo Zone Competition was renamed the Cairo League, but otherwise remained mostly unchanged until the mid-1940s. The EFA felt that a national league, rather than many local leagues, was necessary. The President of the EFA passed the idea on to [[King Farouk I]], who was an avid football fan. The Egyptian Premier League was founded by royal decree, and began play in 1948.<ref name="life" />

===Early years (1948–1960)===
The first match, played on 22 October, was between [[Zamalek SC|Zamalek]] and [[Al Masry SC]], and finished in a 5–1 victory for Zamalek. The match featured the first goal in the Egyptian Premier League, scored by Zamalek's Mohamed Amin, and the first [[hat-trick]] in the new league, by Zamalek's Saad Rustom.<ref>{{cite web |author=Tarek Said |url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/egy49.html |title=Egypt 1948/49 |work=RSSSF |year=2018 |access-date=15 Jun 2022 |archive-date=29 September 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220929212647/https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/egy49.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

[[File:Al Ahly 1948.png|thumb|left|The Al Ahly squad that won the first Egyptian Premier League season.]]

During this time, Premier League results in matches between clubs from Cairo were counted as Cairo League results as well. The Cairo League ceased play in the 1952–53 season and was once played again in the 1957–58 season and cancelled after that season with a narrow difference in the list of title winners between [[Cairo]] rivals, 15 titles for Al Ahly and 14 titles for Zamalek. Zamalek was focused on the Cairo League, winning three consecutive titles from 1949 to 1952, while Al Ahly was dominating the newly born Egyptian League. Despite the importance for this league, few informed and statistics are available.<ref name="list" /> 

Few players rose to prominence in Egyptian football in the 1950s, such as; [[El-Sayed El-Dhizui]], [[Essam Baheeg]], [[Saleh Selim]], [[Hanafy Bastan]], [[Yakan Hussein]], [[Ahmed Mekkawi]], [[Sharif El-Far]], [[Rifaat El-Fanagily]], [[Alaa El-Hamouly]], [[Ad-Diba]], [[Abdou Noshi]], [[Mahmoud El-Gohary]], [[Nour El-Dali]] and [[Adel Hekal]]. Al Ahly won the first three competitions, though in [[1949–50 Egyptian Premier League|1949–50]] they required a playoff against [[Tersana SC]].<ref>{{cite web |author=Tarek Said |url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/egy50.html |title=Egypt 1949/50 |work=RSSSF |year=2007 |access-date=15 Jun 2022 |archive-date=4 December 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221204155133/https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/egy50.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The league was not contested during what would have been the 1951–52 season, as [[Egypt national football team|Egypt's national team]] were competing in the [[Football at the 1952 Summer Olympics|1952 Summer Olympics]]. The season also did not take place due to the [[Egyptian revolution of 1952|1952 Egyptian revolution]], in which [[Farouk of Egypt|King Farouk]] was overthrown. Farouk had allowed his name to be used by his favourite club, which quickly renamed itself Zamalek after the revolution.

[[Gamal Abdel Nasser]], who led the coup and took power after Farouk, was a supporter of Al Ahly, and was named club's honorary president soon after he came to power. This increased the intensity of the already fierce [[Cairo derby]] between Al-Ahly and Zamalek.<ref>{{cite web |author=Gary Parkinson |url=https://www.fourfourtwo.com/features/fourfourtwos-50-biggest-derbies-world-no10-al-ahly-vs-zamalek |title=FourFourTwo's 50 Biggest Derbies in the World, No.10: Al Ahly vs Zamalek |work=[[FourFourTwo]] |year=2016 |access-date=15 Jun 2022 |archive-date=6 August 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190806220936/https://www.fourfourtwo.com/features/fourfourtwos-50-biggest-derbies-world-no10-al-ahly-vs-zamalek |url-status=live }}</ref> Al Ahly won the competition every season until the 1959–60 season, with the majority in a narrow difference with Zamalek. The 1954–55 season was even stopped when Al Ahly conflicted with the [[Egyptian Football Association]] and withdrew. No title was awarded.

===Turbulent Times (1960–1974)===
In the [[1959–60 Egyptian Premier League|1959–60 season]], Zamalek finally won their first title after consistently being runners-up, and Tersana were runners-up and Al Ahly finished third. Zamalek won three titles this decade with the help of a new generation led by [[Hamada Emam]], [[Nabil Nosair]], [[Raafat Attia]], [[Abdou Noshi]], [[Samir Qotb]], [[Yaken Zaki|Yakan Hussein]], [[Ahmed Rifaat]], [[Mahmoud Abou-Regaila]] and others. 

[[File:Zamalek SC (1964).jpg|thumb|The Zamalek squad that won the 1963–64 Egyptian Premier League season.]]

Al Ahly's grip on the league loosened; though they did win some titles, in the [[1965–66 Egyptian Premier League|1965–66 edition]], they finished in 6th out of 12, closer to relegation than to the championship.<ref>{{cite web |author=Tarek Said |year=2007 |title=Egypt 65/66 |url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/egy66.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221126181152/https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/egy66.html |archive-date=26 November 2022 |access-date=15 Jun 2022 |work=RSSSF}}</ref> The decade had five different champions: [[Ismaily SC|Ismaily]] won their first title during this decade, and [[Olympic Club (Egypt)|El-Olympi]] and [[Tersana SC|Tersana]] won the league for the only time. Also, the [[1962–63 Egyptian Premier League|1962–63]] and [[1963–64 Egyptian Premier League|1963–64]] seasons featured 24 teams, a higher number than ever before.<ref name="list" />

The league ceased play in 1967 due to the [[Six-Day War]] and league play was not resumed until the 1971–72 season. In 1969, Ismaily were allowed to play in the [[CAF Champions League]] (then the African Cup of Champion Clubs) as the most recent champions (1966–67 champions).{{efn|They won in the [[1966–67 Egyptian Premier League|1966–67 season]].}} They became the first Egyptian club to win that competition in [[1969 African Cup of Champions Clubs|1969]], though both Al Ahly and Zamalek have now won it many more times.<ref name="CAF">{{cite web |author=Stephen Halchuk, Neil Morrison and Karel Stokkermans |url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablesa/af1.html |title=African Champions' Cup |work=RSSSF |year=2022 |access-date=16 Jun 2022 |archive-date=23 September 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220923155511/https://www.rsssf.org/tablesa/af1.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

In 1971, the league was restarted, only to be swiftly suspended again due to fighting at a match between Al Ahly and Zamalek. A controversial penalty for Zamalek which was scored by [[Farouk Gaafar]], this resulted in a pitch invasion from Al Ahly fans after Al Ahly goalkeeper Marwan Kanafany asked the fans to protest, and the dispute was so intense that the league was not allowed to continue, and no winner was declared, despite Zamalek finishing 1st in the table.<ref name="passion">{{cite web |author=Alaa Al Aswany |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2014/04/17/opinion/egypts-enduring-passion-for-soccer.html |title=Egypt's Enduring Passion for Soccer |work=[[New York Times]] |year=2014 |access-date=15 Jun 2022 |archive-date=16 June 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220616031058/https://www.nytimes.com/2014/04/17/opinion/egypts-enduring-passion-for-soccer.html |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Ghazl El Mahalla SC|Ghazl El Mahalla]] won the league in the [[1972–73 Egyptian Premier League|1972–73 season]] for the only time in their history, but the league was then suspended again for the 1973–74 season because of the [[Yom Kippur War]], and replaced with the [[October League Cup]], which was played once and won by Zamalek.<ref name="list" />

===Post-war Period (1974–2002)===
In the 1970s, a new generation of talented players emerged in the Egyptian football such as; [[Hassan Shehata]], [[Mahmoud El Khatib]], [[Taha Basry]], [[Ekramy El-Shahat|Ekramy]], [[Farouk Gaafar]], [[Abdel Aziz Abdel Shafy]], [[Moustafa Abdou]], [[Hassan El-Shazly|Hassan El Shazly]], [[Ali Khalil]], [[Ali Abo Gresha]], [[Mussad Nur]] and others. Although this generation did not achieve positive results with their country's national team in the 1970s, the league was a strong tournament and full of talent. 

After the [[Yom Kippur War]], Al Ahly won three championships straight, followed by a single championship for Zamalek. This pattern would continue until 1990: Al Ahly would win many championships, followed by a single win for Zamalek, who won their single title in the [[1977–78 Egyptian Premier League|1977–78 season]] with a narrow lead over Al Ahly. This was only interrupted by [[Al Mokawloon Al Arab SC|Al Mokawloon]] winning the 1982–83 edition. This is the latest time a team has won the League for the first time.<ref name="list" /> The league returned to its 24-team format for the [[1975–76 Egyptian Premier League|1975–76 season]], but it quickly reverted to a format featuring between 12 and 16 teams. Tersana were close of winning the [[1974–75 Egyptian Premier League|1974–75 season]] and lost in the final week to Al Ahly with a narrow difference. Top goal scorers fluctuated between [[Hassan Shehata]], [[Mahmoud El Khatib]] and [[Ali Khalil]]. The 1978–79 season was a 12-team format. 

In the 1980s, selected foreign players were chosen to play in the league such as Zamalek's [[Emmanuel Quarshie]], and Al Mokawloon's [[Joseph-Antoine Bell]], until the rules were changed with new limitations on foreign players in 1985. Zamalek and Al Ahly also dominated the [[CAF Champions League]], starting with a [[1982 African Cup of Champions Clubs|1982 triumph]] for Al Ahly, and Zamalek in [[1984 African Cup of Champions Clubs|1984]] and [[1986 African Cup of Champions Clubs|1986]], followed by [[Afro-Asian Club Championship]] for Zamalek in [[1987 Afro-Asian Club Championship|1987]], which Al Ahly won the next year. Al Ahly also won the [[African Cup Winners' Cup]] for three consecutive times from 1984 to 1986. Al Mokawloon won the [[1983 African Cup Winners' Cup]] as well. The Egyptian Premier League became the most successful league in that tournament when Zamalek won in [[1993 African Cup of Champions Clubs|1993]], followed by their win in the [[CAF Super Cup]] over Al Ahly in [[1994 CAF Super Cup|1994]], with two Egyptian contenders for the first time in a continental final.<ref name="CAF" />

The league was not played in 1990 because of Egypt's qualification for the [[1990 FIFA World Cup|1990 World Cup]]. After this delay, Ismaily won the 1990–91 season, followed by Zamalek winning twice in a row in the [[1991–92 Egyptian Premier League|1991–92]] and [[1992–93 Egyptian Premier League|1992–93 seasons]], and after that Al Ahly won every season until the turn of the century. Zamalek and Ismaily briefly rose in power once again between 2000 and 2004, with Zamalek winning [[2000–01 Egyptian Premier League|2000–01]], [[2002–03 Egyptian Premier League|2002–03]] and [[2003–04 Egyptian Premier League|2003–04]] seasons and Ismaily's [[2001–02 Egyptian Premier League|2001–02]] win is the latest time that a team other than Zamalek and Al Ahly have won the title.<ref name="list" />

===Normalcy, then Disaster (2002–2013)===
Between 2004 and 2011, Al Ahly won every edition of the Egyptian Premier League, occasionally being challenged by Zamalek or Ismaily. They also continued to dominate the CAF Champions League, becoming the most successful team in the competition.

The league was one of the strongest and best-attended in Africa,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://dailynewsegypt.com/2022/04/09/egyptian-derbies-you-dont-want-to-miss/ |title=Egyptian Derbies You Don't Want to Miss |work=[[Daily News Egypt]] |year=2022 |access-date=15 Jun 2022 |archive-date=16 June 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220616031058/https://dailynewsegypt.com/2022/04/09/egyptian-derbies-you-dont-want-to-miss/ |url-status=live }}</ref> ranking near the top of the [[CAF 5-year ranking]] since its inception. In 2011, [[2011 Egyptian revolution|another revolution]] began, part of the [[Arab Spring]], which eventually resulted in the overthrow of [[Hosni Mubarak]]. Football featured heavily in the popular uprising, as ultras from clubs such as Al Ahly took part in the revolution.<ref name="passion" />

===Behind Closed Doors (2013–2021)===

The [[2012–13 Egyptian Premier League|2012–13 season]] was cancelled as a result of the [[2013 Egyptian coup d'état]]. After this, the Egyptian Premier League gradually returned to power. Al Ahly has won most seasons since 2013, and have also won two CAF Champions Leagues. Zamalek has won two league titles as well. An attempted return of fans was cancelled when a riot at a match between Zamalek and [[ENPPI SC|ENPPI]] resulted in 19 deaths.<ref>{{cite web |author=Aswat Masriya |url=https://egyptianstreets.com/2015/02/08/30-killed-in-egypt-stadium-riots/ |title=19 Killed In Egypt Stadium Violence |work=[[Egyptian Streets]] |year=2015 |access-date=16 Jun 2022 |archive-date=16 June 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220616144111/https://egyptianstreets.com/2015/02/08/30-killed-in-egypt-stadium-riots/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Fans were finally going to be let back into stadiums when the [[COVID-19 pandemic]] began, delaying the return until 2021.<ref name="return" />

===Partial fans return (since 2021)===
At the beginning of the [[2021–22 Egyptian Premier League|2021–22 season]], 2000 fans were allowed in every match (1000 per team). The situation was getting better so in May 2022 the number increased to 5000 (2500 per team). The season witnessed an improvement of the Egyptian Premier League, the appearance of teams such as: Cairo-based [[Future FC (Egypt)|Future FC]] and Alexandria-based [[Pharco FC]] made the league more challenging and entertaining.

Zamalek defended their title after they won the 2021–22 edition of the league, while Al Ahly witnessed a mass deterioration and even finished the league in third place (behind Pyramids FC and Zamalek) to be out of the top two since 1992 when the club ended the league in the 4th place. Ismaily was on the verge of relegation to the second division but the club eventually managed to improve its results and finished the season in 9th place, while the newly founded Future FC finished in 5th place and managed to qualify for the CAF Confederation Cup, as it was in fourth place in most of the 2021–22 season but lost the position to [[Tala'ea El Gaish SC|Tala'ea El Gaish]] right at the end of the season. At the start of the 2022–23 season, 3000 fans per team were allowed to attend matches.

== Trophy ==
The Egyptian Premier League shield was unveiled in 2023,<ref>{{Cite news |date=2023-07-10 |title=New Design for the Egyptian Premier League Trophy |url=https://www.eparena.com/new-design-for-the-egyptian-premier-league-trophy/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240203220417/https://www.eparena.com/new-design-for-the-egyptian-premier-league-trophy/ |archive-date=3 February 2024 |access-date=2025-06-26 |work=إي بي أرينا |language=ar-AR |url-status=live }}</ref> crowning Al Ahly as its first-ever winners at the end of the 2023/24 season. The shield was designed and manufactured by London-based trophy designers and makers [[Thomas Lyte]].

The shield's design features the wings of Maat, the goddess of truth, justice and fair play. The trophy includes a sundial to symbolise Egypt's connections to cosmology, and a series of motifs inspired by ancient Egyptian art and architecture.

==Competition format and sponsorship==
===Competition===

There are 18 clubs in the Egyptian Premier League. The season lasts from August to May. During the course of the season, each club plays the others twice, once at their home stadium and once at that of their opponents, for a total of 34 games. Teams receive three points for a win and one point for a draw. No points are awarded for a loss. Teams are ranked by total points, then the head-to-head record between the teams in question, then goal difference, and then goals scored.
At the end of each season, the club with the most points is crowned champion. If points are equal, the head-to-head record between the teams in question, then goal difference, and then goals scored determine the winner. At the end of the season, the three lowest-placed teams are relegated into the Egyptian Second League. The Egyptian Second League consists of three groups; the winner of each group is promoted. This system has been around since 2015; before then, the number of teams and relegation places was variable.

===Sponsorship===
The Egyptian Premier League has been sponsored since 2005. The sponsor has been able to determine the league's sponsorship name. The list below details who the sponsors have been and what they called the competition:

==Media coverage==
As the two most powerful clubs, Al Ahly and Zamalek were, before 2014, allowed to negotiate their own television deals. This allowed them to gain the largest television revenue of any club. In 2014, the league negotiated a £E 70,000,000 ($10,160,000) deal with the state-owned Nile Sport Network. However, the deal still guaranteed a great deal of money for Al Ahly and Zamalek, with 10% of revenue going to the team that had won the most Egyptian Premier Leagues (which is, comfortably, Al Ahly), and 10% going to the teams who appeared on television most frequently. Still, the deal did break the tradition of allowing the two clubs to negotiate deals that produced far more profit than the rest of the clubs in the league.<ref name="Economics" />

In 2016, ON Sport TV was granted the rights to televise Egyptian Premier League games. The network is part of the state-owned Egyptian Media Group, which also controls EPL sponsor Presentation Sports.<ref>{{cite web |author=George Mikhail |url=https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2019/01/egypt-soccer-monopoly-state-presentation-caf-afcon-league.html |title=Who controls Egypt's sports scene? |work=[[Al-Monitor]] |year=2019 |access-date=16 Jun 2022 |archive-date=16 June 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220616223818/https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2019/01/egypt-soccer-monopoly-state-presentation-caf-afcon-league.html |url-status=live }}</ref> On Sport launched TIME SPORTS to televise the 2019 Africa Cup of Nations which was hosted by Egypt, right and after the end of the tournament, ON sport TV merged with TIME SPORTS and became known as ON TIME Sports.
