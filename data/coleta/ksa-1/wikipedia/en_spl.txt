The '''Saudi Pro League''' ('''SPL'''), also known as the '''Roshn Saudi League''' ('''RSL'''; {{Langx|ar|دوري روشن السعودي}}) for sponsorship reasons, is a professional [[association football]] league in [[Saudi Arabia]] and the highest level of the [[Saudi football league system]]. The league is governed by the [[Saudi Arabian Football Federation]].

==History==

===Early history (1957–2022)===
The origins of the Saudi top division date back to the establishment of the His Majesty's League in 1957, which continued until 1974. It was followed by the [[1974–75 Saudi Categorization League]], a transitional season before the introduction of the Saudi Premier League. The first official season of the Saudi Premier League was the [[1976–77 Saudi Premier League|1976–77 season]].

The league used a [[round-robin tournament]] format from its first season until the [[1989–90 Saudi Premier League|1989–90 season]]. Following changes by the [[Saudi Arabian Football Federation]], the league was merged with the [[King's Cup (Saudi Arabia)|King's Cup]] and the Golden Box format was introduced. The format included a final knockout stage involving the top four teams from the regular season to decide the champion.

The round-robin format returned in the [[2007–08 Saudi Premier League|2007–08 season]]. The competition was renamed the Saudi Pro League in [[2008–09 Saudi Pro League|2008]], while records from previous league formats continued to be maintained separately.

The league has used several names throughout its history. For the [[2018–19 Saudi Pro League|2018–19]] and [[2019–20 Saudi Pro League|2019–20]] seasons, it was named the Prince Mohammad bin Salman League in honour of [[Crown Prince of Saudi Arabia|Crown Prince]] [[Mohammed bin Salman]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Saudi Football Professional League renamed after Crown Prince for second season |url=https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/574997 |website=Saudi Gazette |date=22 August 2019 |access-date=5 October 2025}}</ref>

In 2019, the General Sports Authority (later transformed into the [[Ministry of Sport (Saudi Arabia)|Ministry of Sport]]) launched the Sports Clubs Support Strategy to improve governance, infrastructure, and fan engagement within Saudi clubs.<ref>{{cite web |title=Saudi Arabia announces ambitious strategy to support sports clubs |url=https://english.ajel.sa/sports/saudi-arabia-announces-ambitious-strategy-to-support-sports-clubs |publisher=Ajel English |access-date=5 October 2025}}</ref>

In 2022, [[Roshn]], owned by the [[Public Investment Fund]], signed a title sponsorship agreement with the league. The competition was renamed the Roshn Saudi League from the [[2022–23 Saudi Pro League|2022–23 season]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Roshn clinches biggest sponsorship deal in Saudi Pro League history |url=https://www.arabnews.com/node/2148991/sport |publisher=Arab News |access-date=5 October 2025}}</ref>

===Saudi Pro League expansion (2023–present)===
In June 2023, the [[Public Investment Fund]] acquired 75% stakes in [[Al-Nassr FC|Al-Nassr]], [[Al Hilal SFC|Al-Hilal]], [[Al-Ittihad Club (Jeddah)|Al-Ittihad]], and [[Al-Ahli Saudi FC|Al-Ahli]] as part of wider development plans linked to [[Saudi Vision 2030]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Walid |first=Ahmed |date=5 June 2023 |title=PIF to take control of Saudi Arabia's four biggest clubs as part of major shake-up in Pro League |url=https://theathletic.com/4581869/2023/06/05/saudi-arabia-pif-pro-league/ |url-access=subscription |access-date=31 July 2023 |work=[[The Athletic]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Why has Saudi sovereign fund taken over kingdom's football clubs? |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2023/6/8/why-has-saudi-sovereign-fund-taken-over-kingdoms-football-clubs |publisher=Al Jazeera |date=June 8, 2023 |access-date=May 8, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Saudi Pro League: the key factors that will decide project's global impact |url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/2023/aug/15/saudi-pro-league-the-key-factors-that-will-decide-projects-global-impact |work=The Guardian |date=August 15, 2023 |access-date=May 8, 2025}}</ref>

The league attracted global attention in 2023 after [[Cristiano Ronaldo]] joined Al-Nassr. Other international players, including [[Neymar]], [[Karim Benzema]], [[Sadio Mané]], and [[N'Golo Kanté]], later joined Saudi clubs during the same period.<ref>{{Cite web |last1=Woodyatt |first1=Amy |last2=Shveda |first2=Krystyna |last3=Cubero |first3=Antonio Jarne |date=2023-09-20 |title=Saudi Arabia is trying to disrupt soccer's world order. The reasons why might surprise you |url=https://edition.cnn.com/2023/09/20/sport/saudi-arabia-soccer-spl-bin-salman-intl-spt-cmd-dg/index.html |access-date=2024-10-05 |website=CNN |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=Saudi wealth shaping football's new reality |language=en-GB |work=BBC Sport |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/66245206 |access-date=2023-08-17}}</ref>

The privatization process continued with several clubs changing ownership. In 2025, [[Al-Kholood Club|Al-Kholood]] became the first Saudi football club to be fully owned by foreign investors after being sold to The Harburg Group, led by American businessman [[Ben Harburg]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.newarab.com/news/first-foreign-firm-takes-ownership-saudi-football-club |title=First foreign firm takes ownership of Saudi football club |publisher=The New Arab |date=24 July 2025 }}</ref>

The league's development has received international attention and criticism. Some observers have described the investments as [[sportswashing]], while Saudi officials have presented the reforms as part of sports development and economic diversification plans.<ref>{{cite web |last=BBC Sport |title=Mohammed bin Salman: 'I don't care' about 'sportswashing' accusations |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/66874723 |date=21 September 2023 |access-date=7 May 2025 |publisher=BBC}}</ref>

==Competition format==
===Competition===
{{updated|the [[2025–26 Saudi Pro League]]}}
There are 18 clubs in the Saudi Pro League. During the course of a season (usually from August to May), each club plays every other club twice (a double round-robin system), once at their home stadium and once at their opponent's, for a total of 34 games. Teams receive three points for a win, one point for a draw, and no points for a loss. Teams are ranked on the league table based on points first, then head-to-head record in case of a tie, followed by goal difference.

Each club is allowed a maximum of 25 players, including up to 10 foreign players. Of these 10 foreign players, 8 can be of any age, and 2 must be under 20 years old at the time of signing. The remaining 15 players must be Saudi nationals, with a maximum of 5 players allowed to come from the youth sector if needed or wanted.

For each league game, managers can select up to 8 foreign players to be included in the matchday squad.

===Promotion and relegation===
A system of promotion and relegation exists between the Saudi Pro League and [[Saudi First Division League]]. The three lowest-placed teams in the Saudi Pro League are relegated to the First Division, in the first division the top two teams are promoted to the Pro League directly, a [[Playoffs#Association football|play-off]] system<ref>{{cite web |title="PLAY-OFF» يحدد صاعدي «يلو" |url=https://arriyadiyah.com/842858/}}</ref> to determine the third team to be promoted alongside the two. Teams placed between third and sixth position take part in the promotion play-offs. The fifth-placed would face the fourth, while the sixth-placed team would face the third. The final would be single-legged, hosted by the higher-placed team.
