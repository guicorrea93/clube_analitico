The '''2005 Campeonato Brasileiro Série A''' (officially the '''Taça [[Nestlé]] Brasileirão 2005''' for sponsorship reasons) was the 50th edition of the [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série A]], a professional league for men's [[Association football|football]] clubs in Brazil. The tournament saw [[Sport Club Corinthians Paulista|Corinthians]] claim their fourth national title. The season officially kicked off on April 23, 2005, and concluded on December 4.

The tournament was heavily marred by a major match-fixing scandal that resulted in the unprecedented invalidation and replay of 11 matches, significantly altering the final standings.

[[Romário]], the veteran striker of [[CR Vasco da Gama|Vasco da Gama]], was the top scorer with 22 goals. 

[[Carlos Tevez]] won the [[Bola de Ouro]] and finished third in the top scorers chart with 20 goals.

==The season==

===Champion and contenders===
Pre-season favorites Corinthians captured their fourth national championship despite a turbulent early season and a campaign that went through 3 different head coaches. Key players [[Carlos Tevez]], [[Carlos Alberto Gomes de Jesus|Carlos Alberto]], [[Roger Galera Flores|Roger]] and [[Gustavo Nery]] led the team to 81 points in 42 games. Despite early season turmoil, Corinthians benefited from being one of the few teams in Brazil who could afford to maintain their talent base throughout the season without having to sell key players. The legitimacy of their title was disputed late in the season due to several scandals on and off the field. As 2005 champions, Corinthians received berth into the first round of the [[Copa Libertadores de América|Libertadores Cup]] as well as the [[Copa Sudamericana|South American Cup]] in 2006.

[[Sport Club Internacional|Internacional]] executed a strong campaign, finishing with a total of 78 points. Coach [[Muricy Ramalho]] led a team with Rafael Sobis, Fernandão, and Tinga to the best performance of any team in the second half, narrowly missing the opportunity for their own fourth national title, but also securing a place in the first round of the Libertadores.<ref>{{Cite web |title= |url=https://portalbrasil.net/2005/esportes/futebol/brasileiro.htm |url-status=live |access-date=2026-06-13 |archive-date=2026-02-11 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260211130359/https://portalbrasil.net/2005/esportes/futebol/brasileiro.htm }}</ref>

Goiás and Palmeiras qualified for the preliminary stage of the 2006 Copa Libertadores for finishing third and fourth respectively. For Goiás, the third-place finish represented the club's best-ever performance in the Série A.

===Consolation prize===
The top middle of the pack was made up of [[Fluminense Football Club|Fluminense]], [[Clube Atlético Paranaense|Atlético/PR]], [[Paraná Clube|Paraná]], [[Cruzeiro Esporte Clube|Cruzeiro]], [[Botafogo de Futebol e Regatas|Botafogo]], [[Santos Futebol Clube|Santos]], and [[São Paulo Futebol Clube|São Paulo]], who were all awarded allocations in the 2006 Copa Sudamericana; with São Paulo, who could not participate due to Libertadores commitments, ceding their spot to 12th placed Vasco da Gama. Santos in particular saw their season nose-dive as star player [[Robinho]] was transferred to [[Real Madrid]] mid-season (a similar fate encountered by Cruzeiro as striker [[Fred (footballer, born 1983)|Fred]] was shipped off to [[Olympique Lyonnais|Lyon]]). Atlético/PR and São Paulo both suffered for having to dedicate their primary attention to the 2005 Libertadores Finals.

===Promotions and relegations===
The system aiming to reduce the total number of participants,<ref>{{Cite web |title=EBC |url=https://memoria.ebc.com.br/agenciabrasil//noticia/2003-10-28/cbf-quer-reduzir-times-na-seria-do-brasileirao |access-date=2026-07-06 |website=memoria.ebc.com.br |language=pt-br}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Folha Online - Especial - 2005 - Campeonato Brasileiro |url=https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/especial/2005/campeonatobrasileiro/regulamento.shtml |access-date=2026-07-06 |website=www1.folha.uol.com.br |archive-date=2006-09-26 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060926073952/http://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/especial/2005/campeonatobrasileiro/regulamento.shtml |url-status=live }}</ref> implemented first in 2004, continued its final year this season, with the bottom four teams relegated to [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série B|Série B]] being [[Clube Atlético Mineiro|Atlético Mineiro]], [[Coritiba Foot Ball Club|Coritiba]], [[Paysandu Sport Club|Paysandu]], and the recently promoted [[Brasiliense Futebol Clube|Brasiliense]]. Série B champions [[Grêmio Foot-Ball Porto Alegrense|Grêmio]] and runner-up [[Santa Cruz Futebol Clube|Santa Cruz]] took their place.

==Controversies==
{{main|2005 Brazilian football match-fixing scandal}}
The 2005 season was severely disrupted by a major refereeing and [[Brazilian football match-fixing scandal|match-fixing scandal]], later known as "Máfia do Apito" (Whistle Mafia). In September 2005, referee [[Edílson Pereira de Carvalho]] was arrested after it was exposed that he had manipulated match outcomes to benefit illegal gambling websites.<ref>{{Cite web |title=EBC |url=https://memoria.ebc.com.br/agenciabrasil//noticia/2005-09-24/cbf-diz-que-vai-pedir-afastamento-de-juiz-preso-pela-policia-federal |access-date=2026-07-06 |website=memoria.ebc.com.br |language=pt-br}}</ref>

As a result of the investigations, the {{ill|Superior Tribunal de Justiça Desportiva|pt}} took the unprecedented decision of invalidating and ordering the replay of 11 matches refereed by Carvalho.<ref>{{Cite news |date=October 2, 2005 |title=Brazilian Soccer Replays Forced by Ref's Fixes |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/2005/10/03/brazilian-soccer-replays-forced-by-refs-fixes/6349a05b-fcde-4885-b9f4-91e34a9dadf5/ |url-access=subscription |url-status=live |work=The Washington Post}}</ref> This decision allowed Corinthians, who replayed two games, to gain crucial points over runners-up Internacional, leading to debates over the fairness of the measures taken.
