In 2009, the '''[[Campeonato Brasileiro Série B]]''', the second level of the Brazilian League, was contested by 20 clubs from May 8 to November 29, 2009. Top four teams in the table would eventually qualify to the [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série A]] to be contested in [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série A 2010|2010]], meanwhile the bottom four would be relegated to [[2010 Campeonato Brasileiro Série C|Série C next season]].

Three former Brazilian champions played in this edition. [[Club de Regatas Vasco da Gama|Vasco da Gama]] was relegated for the first time in history, meanwhile [[Esporte Clube Bahia|Bahia]] and [[Guarani Futebol Clube|Guarani]] came back from [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série C|Série C]] in 2007 and 2008, respectively. Also playing for the first time was [[Duque de Caxias Futebol Clube|Duque de Caxias]], which was promoted along with [[Atlético Clube Goianiense|Atlético Goianiense]] and [[Campinense Clube|Campinense]]. After spending a single season in [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série A|Série A]], [[Ipatinga Futebol Clube|Ipatinga]] and [[Associação Portuguesa de Desportos|Portuguesa]] had returned to Série B along with [[Figueirense Futebol Clube|Figueirense]], which was playing top-level since 2002.

In the beginning of the tournament, [[Club de Regatas Vasco da Gama|Vasco]] was figuring in the middle of the table with 4 draws in 6 matches. Meanwhile, recently promoted [[Guarani Futebol Clube|Guarani]] had a promising start. However, another team which came from [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série C 2008|Séric C]], [[Atlético Clube Goianiense|Atlético Goianiense]], managed to reach the top of the table. But when 4-time Brazilian champions woke up, no other team could stop them. [[Club de Regatas Vasco da Gama|Vasco]] took the lead in the end of the first half of the championship and never let it go. On round 34, after defeating [[Esporte Clube Juventude|Juventude]] 2−1 in a packed [[Estádio do Maracanã|Maracanã Stadium]], [[Club de Regatas Vasco da Gama|Vasco]] clinched their promotion. Two rounds later, in a crowded [[Estádio do Maracanã|Maracanã Stadium]] again, the club from [[Rio de Janeiro]] reached the title after a 2−1 win against [[América Futebol Clube (RN)|América de Natal]]. On round 37, [[Guarani Futebol Clube|Guarani]] and [[Atlético Clube Goianiense|Atlético Goianiense]] also reached [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série A|Série A]] along with [[Ceará Sporting Club|Ceará]], which did not start well but managed comeback and reach the top-four group.

In the other side of the table, teams from Northeastern Brazil struggled to avoid relegation. [[Campinense Clube|Campinense]] stayed in the bottom four group during all the championship and their relegation was confirmed on round 37. In the very same day, [[ABC FC|ABC]] and [[Fortaleza Esporte Clube|Fortaleza]] also booked their trip to [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série C|Série C]], meanwhile 1988 Brazilian champions [[Esporte Clube Bahia|Bahia]] avoided relegation after flirting with it in the last rounds. The last matchday decided who was the last relegated team. [[Brasiliense Futebol Clube|Brasiliense]], [[Ipatinga Futebol Clube|Ipatinga]] and [[América Futebol Clube (RN)|América de Natal]] good results sent [[Esporte Clube Juventude|Juventude]] to the third level of Brazilian football after spending 15 years in Séries [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série A|A]] and [[Campeonato Brasileiro Série B|B]].

==Format==
For the fourth consecutive season, the tournament was played in a double round-robin system. The team with most points has been declared champions. Top four clubs ascended to [[2010 Campeonato Brasileiro Série A|Série A]], meanwhile the bottom four were relegated to [[2010 Campeonato Brasileiro Série C|Série C]].
